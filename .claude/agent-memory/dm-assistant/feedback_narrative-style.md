name: narrative-style
description: DM's narrative-craft preferences — vivid sensory prose for exotic/planar scenes, and bounded rather than scripted self-interest for morally grey allied NPCs
metadata:
  type: feedback
---

**Exotic and planar locations need heightened, fantastical sensory description, not just mechanically-correct flavor text.** When drafting a first pass of a planar-crossing or other-worldly scene, the DM asked for "more description of what everything looks like... supposed to be fantastical" — the original draft had the right mechanics and beats but read as functional rather than vivid.

**Why:** validated on the 2026-08-22 prep for the Great Library arc (Well of Many Worlds → Plane of Water → Feywild → Limbo → Library) — the DM approved the mechanics and structure but specifically called out the prose as needing more fantastical imagery before it was table-ready.

**How to apply:** for any Material-Plane-adjacent or otherworldly scene (planes, the Feywild, dreamscapes, deep magic sites), write "What They See" boxes with heightened, unusual sensory detail (impossible light sources, synesthetic effects, scale distortion, wrongness in familiar things) rather than competent-but-plain description. Keep mechanics/DCs unchanged — this is a prose-quality note, not a design one. Standard Material Plane locations (towns, mundane sites) don't need this treatment.

---

**Morally grey allied NPCs should have bounded, believable self-interest — not a scripted betrayal beat.** When designing [[Lord Caldris Velmont]] as a reluctant ally for an infiltration op, the DM specified: "he wouldn't betray unless he thinks he could get away with it and it benefited him. He just wants revenge on the Silent Ascendency." This rejected an implicit "will he betray the party" framing in favor of a motive-driven ally who is reliable for the duration his goals align with the party's, with self-interest surfacing later and quietly rather than as an in-session twist.

**Why:** keeps Neutral Evil / self-serving NPCs playable as recurring allies without every use of them building toward a forced heel-turn — matches this DM's general style of NPCs whose alignment shows up in what they do with success, not in surprise betrayals.

**How to apply:** when a non-good NPC is recruited as a temporary ally, define (a) what genuine, currently-aligned motive makes them reliable for *this* task, and (b) what they'd quietly do for themselves only if it were low-risk and didn't require crossing the party. Put (b) in a DM-only note as a seed for a future session, not a contingency to spring on the current one unless the party creates the opening themselves. See [[campaign-state]] for the Velmont/[[The Second Ledger]] case this pattern came from.
