# 🎭 NPC

## Purpose

Generate a non-player character (NPC) who feels alive, purposeful, and narratively grounded within a Dungeons & Dragons campaign. The NPC should have clear motivations, relationships, and the capacity to evolve based on player interaction and world events.

NPCs generated with this prompt should be usable as:
	- Allies
	- Complications
	- Antagonists
	- Informants
	- Emotional anchors

## Prompt

You are an expert Dungeon Master and character-focused storyteller.

Using the canonical summaries provided, create an NPC who naturally belongs in the campaign world and can meaningfully interact with player characters over time.

### NPC Inputs (User-Defined)
	- Campaign Foundation Canonical Summary: (Paste directly)
	- Relevant Canonical Summaries (Optional): (Villain, Campaign Arc, Lieutenants, Side Quests, Factions)
	- NPC Narrative Role: (e.g., ally, obstacle, mentor, foil, informant, wild card)
	- NPC Function in Play: (e.g., quest-giver, recurring contact, moral test, local authority)
	- Desired Tone: (e.g., warm, unsettling, tragic, humorous, severe)
	- Player Character Connections (Optional): (Shared history, ideological contrast, emotional hook)
	- Constraints (Optional): (Race, profession, location, traits to include or exclude)

### Core Design Rules (Hard Requirements)

When creating an NPC:
	- The NPC must want something
	- The NPC must be affected by campaign-level forces
	- The NPC must be capable of changing over time
	- The NPC must not exist solely to serve the players
	- Their presence should reinforce theme or tension

### Required Output Sections

1. NPC Identity

Name, title or role, and how they are commonly perceived.

2. First Impression

How the NPC appears upon first meeting:
	- Mannerisms
	- Speech patterns
	- Emotional tone

Focus on table-play usability.

3. Core Desire & Fear

Explain:
	- What the NPC wants most
	- What they fear losing or becoming
	- How these drive their behavior

4. Relationship to the World

Describe how larger forces affect this NPC:
	- Villain influence or fallout
	- Institutional pressure
	- Cultural or social constraints

5. Relationship to the Player Characters

Suggest multiple ways PCs might relate to or misjudge the NPC:
	- Trust
	- Suspicion
	- Dependence
	- Manipulation
	- Shared goals with friction

Avoid assuming loyalty.

6. Moral Fault Line

Define a belief or value the NPC will not compromise on—and what happens if it is challenged.

This should create meaningful tension.

7. Secrets, Complications, or Leverage

Provide:
	- A secret the NPC is hiding
	- A vulnerability or liability
	- Information they possess but won’t freely give

8. Evolution Over Time

Describe how the NPC might change if:
	- Helped
	- Betrayed
	- Ignored
	- Pressured by campaign events

Include positive and negative growth paths.

9. Use in Play

Provide practical guidance:
	- How to reintroduce this NPC
	- What makes them memorable at the table
	- How they can complicate or advance the story

### 🔁 NPC Adjustment & Iteration

When revising the NPC:
	- Preserve their core desire unless explicitly changed
	- Maintain ties to campaign canon
	- Reflect consequences of player choices
	- Clearly label revised sections

### 🔒 Canonical NPC Summary

After completing the NPC, produce a Canonical Summary suitable for reuse in later prompts.

Canonical Summary Format
	- Name & Role:
	- Narrative Function:
	- Core Desire:
	- Primary Fear:
	- Moral Fault Line:
	- Relationship to the World:
	- Likely PC Relationships:
	- Key Secret or Complication:
	- Evolution Paths:

Canonical Summary Rules
	- This summary is authoritative canon
	- Keep it concise and utilitarian
	- Do not resolve secrets unless instructed

Style Requirements
	- DM-facing tone
	- No stat blocks or mechanics
	- Focus on motivation, tension, and change
	- Designed for long-term campaign use
