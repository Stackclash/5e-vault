  As your game advances from low level to high level, one of the big things that you'll want to change is the scope of the story and the plot. The Dungeon Master Guide describes tiers of play on pages 36 through 37, to represent this idea of the story and adventures gaining in scope. ^991844481

[View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kjcx3xfsqn2y1djddrp17ezp)

---

  Now, the first tier of play is levels one through four, where you are local heroes. The PCs don't have a whole lot of power yet, or magic or influence or fame or any of that. So therefore, the adventures should be small in scope, they should be local and against weaker monsters. They should be doing things like helping out a small village, delving into sewers to take care of some monsters, like kobolds, that are bothering the populace, or catching rats. There shouldn't be anything at all epic at this point. ^991844498

[View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kjcx4f5nm7gv7zkgmdtjx87h)

---

  Now, the second tier of play is levels five through ten, where your PCs are considered heroes of the realm. Your PCs are going to get access to a lot more power, and they're going to have good magic spells, like fireball, and magic items, political influence, and most of the people in the local area are going to know who they are. At this point, the adventures you make should start to be things of much higher importance and the scope should include bigger and more powerful monsters, not necessarily bigger, bigger as in terrifying, powerful, deadly. Yeah, you got it. And the stakes should be higher.

If the PCs fail, it's not just that the innkeeper continues to have rats in his cellar. No, if the PCs fail, entire towns might get wiped out, or giants annex an entire region and make it their vassal. Here are some examples of adventures PCs might go on at this level. You might have rampaging orcs, which are evil, by the way, (laughing) that are laying waste to farmhouses outside a major city. You could have Yuan-ti trying to reestablish their empire from the heart of a swamp, or you could have demons gaining a foothold on the plane from the ruins of a desolated fortress. ^991844619

[View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kjcx92bktk58qx31rq66gbad)

---

  All right, levels 11 through 16 are considered higher levels. (laughing) The PCs, the characters, are known here as masters of the realm. Here, your PCs are going to have access to some of the most powerful spells, magic items, and class abilities. They're going to start gaining great political influence from all the adventures they've been on, all the people they're helping, and all the rulers who are thankful to them. All of this gives them political influence, and most people across the world may have heard of them by this point. There are few other adventuring parties that are this powerful. Your players' PCs are now the big kahunas, and even kings and queens show them respect and honor. ^991844870

  So I'm going to go over six things I've personally learned from running campaigns at higher levels. ^991861364

---

  Number one, the encounter building rules are messed up. It doesn't take long for our dungeon master To figure this out, let's just be honest. ^991861389

[View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kjd2z687ysq5wxabg8x9mjqw)

---

  Number two, constantly adjust the game as the PCs gain power and levels. As the PCs level up and gain access to more powerful abilities and spells, you'll need to be on the lookout for ways to adjust your game in response to these. ^993953576

[View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kk0hr722tx6txj1npgyhftc8)

---

  Number three, bad guys aren't idiots. Something else to remember as you design adventures for the higher levels: powerful villains often don't become powerful or stay powerful for long by being complete imbeciles. Even if they don't have book smarts, they have street smarts. This means they anticipate the abilities and spells and magic items that powerful adventurers might have access to. Powerful villains prepare to confront PCs with counterspell, summoning spells, and teleport. ^994020735

[View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kk16waphyjtdn6b10p1n3ncf)

---

  Number four: improvise on the fly. The fact of the matter is this: as the dungeon master, you can't plan everything in advance, so be ready to improvise during a game session in response to your players. ^994020989

[View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kk170p19hyttdjy6edyep3v3)

---

  Number five: focus on good encounter design. At low levels, you might be able to get away with just plopping some monsters into the middle of the room and calling it a day. However, at higher levels, you've really got to do a whole lot better than that. You need to start using terrain, waves, environmental effects, and other elements to improve your combat encounters. ^994021874

[View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kk178a5s2m9dqyfjw99208pq)

---

  Number six: high-level PCs are practically unkillable. You see, even death has little meaning for PCs unless it's a TPK. At high levels, PCs have access to raise dead, resurrection, and even reincarnation; that's a fun one, by the way. ^994022503
