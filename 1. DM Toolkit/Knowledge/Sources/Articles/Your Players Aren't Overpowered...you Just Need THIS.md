Summary: Your players aren't too powerful; the game feels easy because they're not losing resources as intended. Narrative-heavy games often skip the grind, removing tension from encounters and making fights feel simple. To fix this, add resource pressure and varied encounter goals to keep the game challenging and exciting.

  D&D is designed for multiple encounters a day. Like not one, not two. game is actually built around the assumption that your party is going to face around 6 to 8 encounters between long rests. Yes, really! that many. And that's because the game was designed around dungeon crawls, like running multiple sessions, if not dozens, going from.

  Resource management is sort of the game's hidden lever, if you will. Right? That resource attrition is The real balancing mechanic of 5E. I would argue that combat as well, like actually creating encounters is another part of that, but that's a whole other conversation. It's not hit points, it's not monster stats. It's what the party still has left to spend. And that's their spell slots, their rages, their bardic inspiration, their superiority dice. All of those things that exist in a system Where players are supposed to feel the pinch over time. ^991839286

[View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kjcw7r2k829fcd0p8212jzs8)

  So let's say you realize that your players are steamrolling everything, and your gut instinct is Probably to crank the dial so you double the HP, you add more actions, You throw in a dragon with maximum damage breath. And that'll shake them up, know, like once, maybe twice. But the problem is,

is that increasing the difficulty isn't the same as increasing tension. What you're actually doing is just stretching out that fight and making it more and more boring, In my opinion. What really matters is Encounter variety, because when every fight is “Roll initiative, kill the bad guys”, then the only way to make It harder is to inflate the numbers. And that doesn't create excitement. It just creates that excessive bloat. And the real tension comes from situational pressure. ^991839411

[View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kjcwba2f5zvb71n6jt2sdk5x)

---

  You don't need to force your players through eight exhausting fights in a row just to play five correctly. You know, you just need to reintroduce that pressure. You need to create friction between them and their power. So I would say start by sprinkling in smaller, low stakes encounters. Not everything needs to be a boss fight, even a pack of wolves. And then a collapsing bridge and then a cursed zone. All of that is going to eat away at HP over time. And burn through their player resources again. ^991840029

[View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kjcwk9y22y8m2pfv0v3sjnxs)

  Now, what's even better is when you can mix in mechanical Pressure with that story pressure. So maybe the party has to stop a ritual before Dawn and then suddenly long rest. Isn't an option because things are moving faster, now your players are having to play smart instead of playing strong, right? Because now you're squeezing those those mechanical, fixes. ^991840909
