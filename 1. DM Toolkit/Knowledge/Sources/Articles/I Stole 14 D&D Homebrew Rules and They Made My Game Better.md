# Document Note

Background: I am a dungeon master who wants to improve my table management, player engagement, and the mechanical and narrative flow of my games by selectively adopting homebrew rules that make play more fun, faster, and more rewarding for new and returning players.

🔧 Make combat and mechanics more player-friendly and cinematic  
- Ease crunch for new players (monster -2 AC/attacks/saves when introducing first-timers).  
- Use team initiative (players roll one for the group; DM rolls monsters; teams alternate turns) to keep engagement and strategy high.  
- Treat crits and hit-down mechanics to feel impactful (max extra dice on crits; new "dying" condition with clear effects; grapples as opportunity attacks).

💬 Boost player agency, morale, and meaningful choices  
- Give one inspiration per player per session to distribute next session; allow gifting and use-after-roll to increase drama.  
- Allow help only when proficient (or require a pitch) so Advantage is earned and meaningful.  
- Let players take a level-1 feat when appropriate to increase build variety and investment.

⏱️ Streamline pacing and recovery while raising stakes  
- Make potion use flexible (bonus action with variable roll; action to get max heal).  
- Control equipment interactions (drawing/sheathing/object interactions and shield don/doff rules) to avoid abuse.  
- Use exhaustion rules tied to hits/zero HP and long rests (zero HP = +1 exhaustion; long rest removes 1, comfortable long rest removes 2; updated exhaustion penalties and death at level 11) to balance tension and recovery.
