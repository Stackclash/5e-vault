# Document Note

Background: I’m a dungeon master who wants to improve my storytelling and encounter design to run more engaging, conflict-driven D&D sessions.

🔥 Make NPC/monster goals clear and opposing
Give every NPC and monster a concrete, timed goal that can conflict with the PCs’ goals. If an NPC’s objectives align with the party, there’s no story — opposition creates meaningful choices and reactions.

⚖️ Treat the game as chains of action → reaction
Frame scenes as a sequence where player actions provoke NPC/monster reactions, which then become new player actions. Design encounters so each reaction leads naturally to another decision until a final reaction closes the scene.

🎯 Focus on goal + stakes + deadline
Ensure scenes satisfy the storytelling formula: someone wants something badly, by a specific time, and is having trouble getting it. Use stakes and deadlines to force tension and prevent easy, story-killing solutions.
