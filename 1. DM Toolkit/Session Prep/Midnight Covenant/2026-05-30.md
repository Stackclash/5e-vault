## Strong Start

> [!info] The Warehouse District — Midnight Rain
> The party arrives at the warehouse as midnight rain hammers the tin rooftops of the Industry District docks. Taking position outside, they spot something wrong — light flickers through cracked shutters, and inside, figures are **urgently loading crates onto a cart**. Someone tipped off the Dustfinger Society that a raid was coming.
>
> Before the party can act, the cart driver spots them. He shouts an alarm.
>
> **The choice is made for them.** Do they rush the loading dock to stop the evidence moving — or let the cart run and follow it to a bigger stash?
>
> *Sensory cues: rain drumming on the tin roof, clattering crates, a faint blue glow from enchantment containment boxes through the shutters, shouted Thieves Cant, the sharp smell of arcane preservatives.*

## Scenes

> [!info] Scene 1 — The Warehouse
> **Location:** Converted three-story grain building, Industry District docks. Rain falls through a broken skylight. Shelves of blue-glowing Athenaeum containment boxes. The sharp smell of arcane preservatives mixed with wet wood and damp rope.
>
> **Tactical features:** Rope ladders between floors (cutting one strands anyone above), stacked crates for cover (can be knocked with a Shove action), a canal trapdoor on the ground floor (DC 13 Athletics to force quickly under pressure — leads to 40ft of dark water running beneath the district).
>
> **Memorable detail:** One cracked containment box holds a minor Cantrip Wand misfiring at random — a small arc of blue-white lightning discharges every 30 seconds, threatening to ignite crates. Nobody has stopped it.
>
> **The locked iron vault (ground floor):** Sealed with Arcane Lock. The key is on Tavish. Contains the current shipment and **Clue 1** — a ledger sealed with a stylized **"V" wound in chain links**. DC 12 History: this is a noble family crest, not an Athenaeum seal.
>
> **Tavish Ironwood if cornered:** He will bargain. He has never seen the Benefactor's face — always a hooded figure who smelled of cedar and enchantment reagents. He kept one letter as insurance pointing to a dead-drop: *"the third stall from the left at the Ashmarket ironmonger."* — **Clue 2.**
>
> **Flesh Golem:** Activates if the vault is forced without the key, or if Tavish shouts the command word *"Grindstone."* Focuses exclusively on threats to the vault. Its Berserk trigger (below 40 HP) may cause it to attack Enforcers as well — lean into that chaos.

> [!info] Scene 2 — The Ashmarket Dead-Drop
> **Location:** Grey-market bazaar near the Artisan District. The "third stall" sells repaired tools as a front, run by **Pell**, a nervous halfling.
>
> Pell relays sealed letters between parties without opening them and has one waiting for pickup now.
>
> **Getting Pell to talk:** Intimidation DC 13 or Persuasion DC 16. Catching him trying to send a warning (Perception DC 14) gives Advantage on either check.
>
> **The Letter — Clue 3:** Uses enchantment theory terminology (*"sympathetic resonance chains," "attuned binding"*) and references delivering items to *"the workshop at the estate on Thornwhistle Lane."* Elegant, aristocratic handwriting. Arcana DC 14: vocabulary is consistent with Enchantment school academic training at the Athenaeum. Investigation DC 12: the paper bears a faint watermark — the same "V in chains" crest as the warehouse ledger.

> [!info] Scene 3 — Velmont's Private Workshop
> **Location:** Modest townhouse on Thornwhistle Lane. Ground floor appears residential. Hidden workshop behind a bookcase — Investigation DC 13 to find the mechanism.
>
> **What's inside:** Stolen items mid-enchantment (Athenaeum catalog numbers still visible on their tags), receipts of sale to three Bruokdon noble houses, and **forged Athenaeum access seals** used to explain how items left the building — the most damning evidence.
>
> **Clue 4:** A contract signed *"C. Velmont"* for *"private academic research materials."* A second document is a signed extraction agreement with The Impenetrable Fortress — emergency planar escape services, prepaid.
>
> **Velmont's sequence if present:**
> 1. **Charm** — opens with conversation; casts Charm Person on the biggest talker. Touches his rings while thinking. Claims they have found *"a misunderstanding he can explain."*
> 2. **Negotiate** — offers 2,000 gp and political protection. Names one noble buyer as a greater villain to deflect blame.
> 3. **Flee** — when below 30 HP or clearly losing, activates the Impenetrable Fortress portal: a brass disc set into the floor under a desk rug. He escapes to the Fortress. He is now a **recurring villain with planar resources and noble backing.**
>
> **If Velmont is not present:** The evidence is all still here. A sending stone on the desk is attuned to Velmont — he becomes aware the party is in his workshop within 10 minutes.

> [!info] Scene 4 — Reporting to the Athenaeum / Receiving the Well
> Professor Gousse Windcaller summons the party to his office when news of the raid reaches him. He is visibly shaken — he believed Velmont ambitious but loyal.
>
> **Council reaction (roleplay moment):**
> - Selvara Kain (Abjuration): outraged, demands immediate action against Velmont and the noble buyers
> - Kaelor Stormbrand (Evocation): wants to go after Velmont personally; offers to join the party
> - Valeris Thorne (Necromancy): unsettlingly calm — *"His absence clarifies certain matters."*
>
> **The Well of Many Worlds:** Gousse produces it now and briefs them on the planar route, sketching it on translucent parchment. Route: **Material Plane → Plane of Air → Positive Energy Plane → The Gray Plain (Sigil) → The Great Library.**
>
> He warns them specifically:
> - *"The Positive Energy Plane will try to heal you to death. Do not let the rapture take hold."*
> - *"In Sigil, find a Githzerai named Tessavar at a shop called The Turning Page. She knows where the Library's portal is. She will want something in return."*
> - *"The Library's Servitors are not mindless. Bring an honest question, not a demand. Vague requests are ignored."*
>
> **Velmont's noble patrons** remain a loose thread — Gousse warns the party these nobles will not accept exposure quietly. A political arc is beginning.

> [!info] Scene 5 (Flexible) — The First Passage Opens
> Can occur in Gousse's study or wherever the party chooses. Use as a session-ending beat if Part 1 runs long, or as a mid-session transition.
>
> The Well of Many Worlds unfolds on the floor like a pool of starless sky. Air pressure drops noticeably. Sounds become muffled, then distant. The pull is unmistakable — like standing at the edge of a very tall cliff with the wind already pulling at your boots.
>
> *Step through, and everything changes.*

### Planar Journey — Framework

> [!info] Planar Stop 1 — The Plane of Air
> **Arrival:** Falling upward into an infinite sky. No floor, no ceiling — just blue-white in every direction. Enormous floating rock islands connected by swaying rope bridges. The passage forward is a howling wind tunnel bored through the center of the largest island, guarded by an Air Elemental Myrmidon.
>
> **Light gravity:** Jump twice as far and high. Carry double before encumbered. Disadvantage on attacks for the first day as the body adjusts.
>
> **Memorable detail:** Standing at the edge of any island and looking "down" is identical to looking "up." There is no horizon. Clouds drift both above and below. A creature that falls simply falls — in any direction — until they catch something or slow themselves.
>
> **Aarakocra air cultists** control this island and oppose outsiders. They attack regardless of what happens with the Myrmidon. (See Monsters.)

> [!info] Planar Stop 2 — The Positive Energy Plane
> **Arrival:** A wave of warmth and overwhelming vitality. Colors are brighter than they should be — the party may feel they are seeing new parts of the spectrum. The sky gives off a constant blinding golden-white glow.
>
> **Mechanical effect (ongoing):** In moderate-energy areas, creatures regain 2 HP per round until at maximum. In the high-energy zone where the Everbright Fury is located, the dangerous overflow mechanic applies (see Monsters).
>
> **Memorable detail:** Wounds close before the party can register them. Old scars fade. A broken fingernail regrows. It feels unconditionally good — which should make them nervous.
>
> **⚠️ Note for Redgrave:** The Sunsword deals radiant damage. Radiant damage heals the Everbright Fury instead of harming it. This encounter is specifically designed to require a different approach.

> [!info] Planar Stop 3 — The Gray Plain & Sigil
> **Arrival in Sigil:** Inside the ring-city above the Spire of the Planes. Gravity pulls toward the city surface — they walk on the inner curve of the ring. Looking through the crystal-slab streets reveals the Spire and gray plains miles below.
>
> **Divine magic fails in Sigil:** Prayers to gods do not function. Channel Divinity tied to a deity is suppressed. Cleric and Paladin spell slots still work but any effect described as divinely granted (DM's discretion) fails. Time is slow: 1 day in Sigil = 3 days on the Material Plane.
>
> **Memorable detail:** The markets contain a modron methodically cataloging street goods next to a githzerai selling bottled memories next to two angels arguing over a secondhand blade. Nobody looks twice. This is simply Sigil.
>
> **The Turning Page Bookshop:** Mid-market district, marked by a sign showing an open book with a door in its pages. Tessavar is always writing in a small notebook and rarely makes direct eye contact.

> [!info] Planar Stop 4 — The Great Library
> **Arrival:** A grand pentagonal foyer. A soundless fountain at its center. Five corridors stretch into apparent infinity — each lined floor to ceiling with bookshelves. The air is dusty but the books are all in pristine condition. Invisible Servitors move silently, returning books to shelves within one minute of being set down.
>
> **Corridors:** I. Magical Theory & Arcana, II. Historical & Religious Literature, III. The Multiverse, IV. Physics, Metaphysics & Everything In Between, V. Mathematics, Architecture & Miscellaneous.
>
> **The restricted section** is below the fountain — locked bookcases accessible only with Servitor permission. This is where the Vecna-relevant material is.
>
> **Light gravity.** Time is very slow: 1 day in the Library = 7 days on the Material Plane. Evocation and Illusion spells have a 20% failure chance. Concentration on Illusion spells has a base DC of 15 instead of 10.
>
> **The warning:** Someone has accessed the restricted section within the last month. The Servitors noted: *"a robed humanoid who left no footprints and whose face no Servitor could recall when asked to describe it."* — the Silent Ascendency has been here before the party.

## Secrets and Clues

> [!tip] 10 Secrets — placeable in any scene
> 1. The "V in chain links" on the warehouse ledger is the Velmont family noble crest — DC 12 History.
> 2. Velmont isn't reselling stolen items wholesale; he's using them as raw enchantment components to craft new items, bypassing Athenaeum oversight entirely.
> 3. Tavish never saw the Benefactor's face — but recalls cedar and enchantment reagents. Anyone who has visited Velmont's Athenaeum office recognizes the smell immediately.
> 4. Velmont's noble buyers have already used some of the enchanted items for magical coercion against city officials. This is bigger than one corrupt councilor. (Hint at a political arc.)
> 5. The warehouse Flesh Golem was built using Athenaeum resources — Velmont signed the material requisition under a false project name. Its Athenaeum catalog number is stamped on its left heel.
> 6. The Greedy Griffon acts as the receiving front for "legitimate purchases" and keeps meticulous records the party could use as corroborating evidence.
> 7. Velmont has a prepaid extraction contract with the Impenetrable Fortress — if cornered, he can flee to another plane and the Fortress will protect him as long as he can pay.
> 8. The Well of Many Worlds can be redirected from *inside* a planar passage once travelers reach an intermediate milestone — useful for skipping ahead or changing destination mid-journey.
> 9. The Great Library's Corridor III contains information about Vecna's required Metaphysical Truth — specifically in the restricted section under the fountain.
> 10. Tessavar in Sigil is not just a bookseller — she is a covert agent of the Great Library. She already knows the party is coming before they arrive.

## Fantastic Locations

> [!info] The Warehouse
> Three-story converted grain building on the Industry District docks. Rain through a broken skylight. Shelves of blue-glowing enchantment containment boxes. Smells of arcane preservatives and wet wood. The iron vault sits in the center of the ground floor — clearly the point of highest security. Rope ladders dangle between levels. The canal trapdoor is flush with the floorboards, easy to miss (Perception DC 13).
>
> **The memorable detail:** The misfiring Cantrip Wand in a cracked containment box — a small arc of blue lightning fires every 30 seconds. It has already scorched one shelf black. If it hits a containment box, that box also cracks and its stored item misfires. A chain reaction is possible.

> [!info] Sigil — City of Doors
> Inside the ring-city above the Spire of the Planes. Gravity pulls toward the city surface — so standing "upright" means standing on what appears to be a curved ceiling. Looking through the crystal-slab paving stones is looking straight down miles of open air to the plains below.
>
> **The memorable detail:** At the exact center of the ring, if the party looks "up" along the ring's axis, they can see the far inner curve of the city — upside-down streets, upside-down buildings, tiny figures going about their day — an entire city reflected back at them in an impossible arc.

> [!info] The Great Library
> Five corridors stretching to apparent infinity from a pentagonal foyer. Thirty-foot vaulted ceilings. Light filtered through floating dust, giving everything a warm amber haze. Books in absolute pristine condition despite the dust. The soundless fountain at the foyer's center has water that flows without sound and reflects not what is above it — but what is on a different shelf elsewhere in the library.
>
> **The memorable detail:** A book the party walks past has their names in it. The title reads: *Accounts of Uncertain Travelers, Volume MXCII.* If they open it, the pages covering their arrival are blank — but the preceding pages describe, in detail, planar travelers who came before them seeking the same restricted section.

## Important NPCs

> [!info] Tavish Ironwood — Dustfinger Society Lieutenant
> **Goal:** Survive and avoid imprisonment.
> **Secret:** He kept one ledger page as insurance naming the dead-drop and bearing the "V" crest — hands it over immediately for a deal.
> **Personality:** Pragmatic street tough who followed the money; no ideological loyalty to the Society.
> **Mannerism:** Picks constantly at his thumbnail when nervous.
> **Will betray Velmont without hesitation** — it was always purely business.

> [!info] Lord Caldris Velmont — Corrupt Councilor of Enchantment
> **Goal:** Escape accountability; maintain his noble patron network.
> **Secret:** Prepaid extraction contract with the Impenetrable Fortress. Has been supplying nobles with items now used for magical coercion against city officials.
> **Personality:** Smooth, persuasive, every gesture calculated in advance. Never shows fear.
> **Mannerism:** Touches his enchanted rings when thinking. Holds eye contact a beat too long — it's not intimidation, it's assessment.
> **What he fears:** Public disgrace and losing his council seat — not physical harm.
> **If he escapes:** Recurring villain with planar resources. The Fortress doesn't care about his crimes — only his contract.

> [!info] Pell — Ashmarket Letter Runner
> **Goal:** Stay uninvolved and alive.
> **Secret:** He's seen the "V in chains" crest on the letters for months and never connected it to anyone specific. He keeps everything.
> **Personality:** Terrified. Talks extremely fast when scared. Hands shake.

> [!info] Tessavar — Githzerai Bookseller, Sigil
> **Goal:** Acquire unique written knowledge for her archive.
> **Secret:** She is one of the Great Library's covert agents in Sigil and has already reported the party's arrival.
> **Personality:** Calm, laconic, professionally curious. Unsurprised by anything.
> **Mannerism:** Always writing in a small notebook mid-conversation; rarely looks directly at the speaker.
> **Her Price:** She gives the party the portal location in exchange for *"a true account of something you witnessed that no one else has written down."* She does not care what it is — the Library wants first-person accounts of unique events.

> [!info] The Air Passage Guardian — Elemental Myrmidon
> **Goal:** Test the worthiness of travelers passing through.
> **The Ritual:** Demands spoken tribute — *"Tell me one true thing you have never said aloud before."* It listens to all air in the Material Plane. A false statement triggers combat. A true one opens the passage.
> **Voice:** Wind through reed grass. Slightly echoing.
> **If bypassed peacefully:** Nods once and dissolves into the wind. The tunnel opens.

> #### Tactics
>
> **Tavish Ironwood — Assassin (INT 13)**
> Round 1: Hidden on floor 2/3 before the party enters. Assassination auto-crit crossbow shot at Wisp (+6 to hit, avg 7 piercing + DC 15 CON save for 7d6 poison). Bonus action: Hide immediately. Shouts "Grindstone!" to activate the Golem on his first turn OR when his HP drops to 55 or below — he watches where the fight clusters before waking it.
> Core loop: Attack from concealment → Bonus Action Hide → reposition along rope ladders. Never stands in the open.
> Escape: At ≤40 HP — Cunning Action Dash toward the canal trapdoor. Next turn: Disengage, drop through, swim out. His escape is a designed setup for a future encounter, not a failure state.
>
> **Flesh Golem (INT 6)**
> Inert until "Grindstone!" command word OR a PC forces/attacks the vault door. Targets nearest creature within 5 ft. of the vault; does not pursue beyond 30 ft. unless Berserk. 2 slam attacks (+7 to hit, 13 bludgeoning each). Immune to nonmagical B/P/S, charmed, frightened, paralyzed, poisoned.
> Cantrip Wand interaction: If the wand's lightning arc hits the Golem, it heals instead (Lightning Absorption). Foreshadow this early — the Golem visibly twitches toward the sparks in round 1.
> Berserk (below 40 HP): Roll d6 each turn — on a 6, attacks the nearest creature regardless of faction. A Berserk golem turning on a Blackguard is the intended chaos finale.
>
> **Blackguards ×2 (INT 11)**
> Priority target: Redgrave. One holds the line; the second breaks toward the Cleric. They want to Blind the healer before it can anchor the fight.
> Core loop: 3 glaive attacks (+7 to hit, reach 10 ft., avg 18 per hit — 9 slashing + 9 necrotic). **Necrotic component bypasses Barbarian rage resistance.** After every hit: Bonus Action Blind Smite — DC 13 CON save or Blinded for 1 minute (Faerah CON +2 = ~50% fail, Wisp CON +0 = ~60% fail).
> Dreadful Aspect when 3+ PCs are within 30 ft.: DC 13 WIS save or Frightened (Roark WIS +0, Yevelda WIS +1 = ~50% fail each). Best used round 2–3 the moment Wisp steps forward to cast.
> They do not retreat. They may use Command (DC 13 WIS) to Grovel, Flee, or Halt PCs with poor WIS saves.
>
> **Master Thieves ×3 (INT 11)**
> Priority: Faerah (they recognize another rogue — double-team her to deny Sneak Attack). Secondary: Wisp (shortbow from range).
> Round 1: Two ground-floor operatives use Cunning Action Hide immediately on the alarm. One second-floor operative readies a shortbow shot. They never cluster within Fireball radius.
> Core loop: 3 attacks (+7 to hit, avg 10 per hit — 1d6+4 piercing + 1d6 poison, no save) → Cunning Action Disengage if hit, Hide if not. Uncanny Dodge reaction halves one hit per round. At ≤40 HP: scatter to different floors via rope ladders. They are not loyal enough to die for Tavish.
>
> ---
>
> #### Combat Flow
>
> **Round 1:** Blackguards already positioned between entry and vault. Master Thieves scatter and Hide (Cunning Action). Tavish takes the Assassination shot at Wisp before Wisp acts. Wisp potentially at single-digit HP on turn one. "Grindstone!" — the Flesh Golem lurches to life.
>
> **Rounds 2–3:** Party splits attention between the Golem (at the vault), two Blackguards with 10 ft. glaive reach and Blind Smite, and three Master Thieves bouncing cover positions with 3 attacks + poison. Cantrip Wand sparks again (initiative count 5). Dreadful Aspect lands during this window.
>
> **Signature Moment — "Grindstone!":** Tavish screams the command word when the party is already engaged. The party must immediately choose: chase Tavish or deal with an immune construct.
>
> **Signature Moment — The Wand Heals the Golem:** Someone tries to redirect the wand's arc at the Golem. It heals instead. The Golem roars, wounds sealing.
>
> **Round 4+:** Tavish flees. Master Thieves scatter upstairs. Blackguards die in place. If Golem goes Berserk, chaotic final act — it turns on whoever is closest.
>
> ---
>
> #### Party Risk Assessment
>
> | PC | Key Risk |
> |---|---|
> | **Wisp** (42 HP) | **EXTREME.** Tavish auto-crit round 1 — avg 16 piercing + 7d6 poison (DC 15 CON, Wisp +0). Potential 40+ damage on first hit. Illusory Self saves once. |
> | **Faerah** (73 HP) | **HIGH.** Master Thief double-team (6 attacks/round on her). Blackguard Blind Smite (CON +2, ~50% fail) — blinded Faerah loses Sneak Attack. |
> | **Redgrave** (73 HP) | **MODERATE.** Primary Blackguard target. Blind Smite (CON +3, ~50% fail) — a blinded Cleric cannot heal effectively. |
> | **Yevelda** (92 HP) | **MODERATE.** Blackguard necrotic damage bypasses rage resistance. Flesh Golem is immune to her attacks unless she has a magical weapon. |

> **⚠️ Wisp (Fireball, DEX save):** Master Thieves have Evasion (+7 DEX save = ~65% take zero damage). Fireball is most effective against the Blackguards (DEX +0). Encourage target prioritization.
> **⚠️ Flesh Golem immunity:** Roark, Yevelda, and Faerah cannot damage the Golem with standard nonmagical attacks. Redgrave (Sun Blade) and Wisp (Fire Bolt, Fireball) are the primary Golem threats.

> [!warning] Encounter 2 — Velmont's Workshop (Hard)
> #### Lord Caldris Velmont — *Mage, CR 6 (MM p.347) — Modified Spell List*
> Replace standard spells with enchantment focus:
> - **3rd level:** Hypnotic Pattern, Counterspell
> - **4th level:** Dominate Person, Greater Invisibility
> - **5th level:** Hold Monster, Dominate Monster (1/day)
>
> He wears the **Shield Guardian Amulet** (see below). When below 30 HP, uses his movement on that turn to reach the brass portal disc under the desk rug and activate it. He vanishes to the Impenetrable Fortress.
>
> #### 1× Shield Guardian — *CR 7 (MM p.271)*
> Bound to Velmont via an amulet he wears visibly. **Stealing the amulet** (Sleight of Hand contested by Velmont's Perception +3; Advantage while he is Concentrating on a spell) immediately stops the Shield Guardian — it cannot attack whoever now holds the amulet.
> *Absorb Damage: once per round, if Velmont takes damage, the Shield Guardian can absorb up to 20 of it.*
>
> #### 2× Homunculus — *CR 0 (MM p.188)*
> Non-combat flavor. Scatter immediately at the first sign of violence. If captured, they have witnessed everything Velmont has done — but cannot speak without his command word.
>
> **Encounter note:** XP math: 5,200 raw × 1.5 multiplier = **7,800 adjusted** (Medium by the book). However, Velmont's modified spell list — Dominate Person, Hold Monster, Greater Invisibility — makes him significantly more disruptive than a base CR 6 Mage. The Shield Guardian's Absorb Damage (20 HP/round) effectively extends Velmont's staying power. In practice this plays closer to Hard, but expect it to hinge entirely on whether the party cracks the amulet puzzle early. The Shield Guardian amulet is the tactical puzzle here. **Faerah (DEX 21, Swashbuckler Rogue, Sleight of Hand proficiency) is the primary pick for the steal attempt** — she has the best Dexterity in the party and can Sneak Attack without an adjacent ally, letting her stay mobile. Note: Velmont's Charmed target (biggest talker) may be Yevelda or Roark — Charmed imposes Disadvantage on attacks against Velmont, effectively removing a Barbarian from damage. Dominate Person on either Barbarian is the most dangerous single move Velmont can make (Roark 137 HP, Yevelda 92 HP turned on the party). Players who figure out the amulet steal will feel smart — let them.

> [!warning] Encounter 3 — Plane of Air: The Wind Tunnel (Hard, fully skippable)
> #### Air Elemental Myrmidon — *CR 7 (Mordenkainen's p.200)*
> Only fights if someone lies during the spoken truth ritual, attacks first, or tries to force past without engaging. **This encounter is designed to be bypassed through roleplay.** Reward honesty.
> *If combat is triggered: uses Whirlwind to throw party members off island edges, targets fliers first, retreats into the wind tunnel where its speed advantage is maximized.*
>
> #### 4× Air Cult Fanatic — *Cult Fanatic, CR 2 (MM p.345)*
> Present regardless of what happens with the Myrmidon — these cultists want to stop outsiders. Use **Dive Attack**: if flying at the start of their turn, their first attack deals an extra 3d6 damage (avg 10). They stay airborne and focus fire on grounded party members.
>
> **Encounter note:** The cultists are the mandatory fight; the Myrmidon is the avoidable one. If the party does both, it's a Hard encounter (9,400 adjusted, just below the 9,500 Hard threshold). If they bypass the Myrmidon socially, it's Medium (the cultists alone). Either is appropriate for Level 10.
>
> **⚠️ Party-specific — light gravity disadvantage:** Disadvantage on attacks affects Roark, Yevelda, and Faerah's shortbow (all attack-roll based). Wisp's Fireball (DEX save) and Redgrave's Spirit Guardians (CON save) are **unaffected** — lean on those for damage output. Faerah's Darkness (Drow Magic) or Faerie Fire is worth setting up early if the Myrmidon is also engaged. The cultists are native to this plane and do not suffer the light gravity penalty.

> [!danger] Encounter 4 — Positive Energy Plane: The Everbright Fury (Hard)
> #### Everbright Fury — *Reskinned Deva, CR 10 (MM p.16)*
> An overloaded positive energy elemental driven mad by radiant saturation. Use the Deva's stat block with the following changes:
>
> **Remove:** Healing Touch, Innate Spellcasting.
>
> **Add:**
> - **Radiant Overflow Aura (20 ft):** Each creature that starts its turn in the aura must make a DC 15 Con save or gain 10 temporary HP. If a creature's temporary HP total ever exceeds their maximum HP, they must make a DC 15 Con save or **detonate** (4d10 radiant, 15 ft radius, DC 13 Dex save for half). On a successful detonation save, they shed half their temp HP as a harmless blinding burst.
> - **Radiant Regeneration:** Regains 10 HP per round. Radiant damage heals it instead of dealing damage.
> - **Overwhelm (Recharge 5–6):** Each creature within 30 ft must make a DC 16 Con save or be Stunned until the end of their next turn from radiant saturation.
>
> **How to stop it:** The Fury cannot be killed conventionally (the regeneration and radiant immunity make that impossible). It must be *calmed* by introducing shadow or necrotic energy. A necrotic damage spell, the *Darkness* spell, or any shadow-aligned ability cast within 10 ft of it suppresses its Radiant Overflow Aura for 1 minute and causes it to hesitate. A DC 17 Arcana check reveals this weakness. The Fury itself mutters fragmented phrases suggesting what it needs: *"...dark...balance...quiet..."*
>
> **Encounter note:** XP math: **5,900 adjusted** (solo Deva = Medium by the book). The "Hard" label is accurate in practice — Radiant Regeneration (10 HP/round) means the party cannot win through attrition, and the Overwhelm stun (Recharge 5–6, DC 16 Con) can lock multiple players. The fight is Hard because the solution must be discovered mid-combat, not because of raw damage output.
>
> **⚠️ Party-specific — Redgrave is almost entirely offline offensively:** Sun Blade (radiant), Sacred Flame (radiant), and Spirit Guardians (radiant) all **heal the Fury**. Redgrave's only non-radiant options are melee weapon attacks with a non-radiant damage source if he swaps — otherwise he should focus on healing, Sanctuary, or debuffs and stay out of damage output entirely. The effective offense here is Roark + Yevelda (nonmagical physical, works fine against this target), Faerah (piercing/slashing, works), and Wisp (Fire Bolt/Fireball, works).
>
> **⚠️ Puzzle solution depends on Faerah's Darkness:** Faerah's Drow Magic Darkness is the only confirmed shadow/darkness source in the party. It is 1/long rest. **Verify before running this encounter that Faerah still has it available.** If she used it earlier, the party's path to suppressing the Aura is DC 17 Arcana (to discover the weakness) plus improvised necrotic options. Also check Wisp's prepared spell list — an Illusion Wizard can prepare spells from any school, and Vampiric Touch or Enervation would solve this immediately. If the party has no necrotic option at all, consider placing a Scroll of Darkness or a shadow-attuned object as a discoverable item in the environment before this encounter.
