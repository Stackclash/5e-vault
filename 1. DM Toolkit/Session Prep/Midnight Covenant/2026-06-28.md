## Strong Start

> [!info] Thornwhistle Lane — Pre-Dawn
> The party arrives at Velmont's townhouse before the city wakes. There is a light on inside — not the diffuse glow of an enchanted lamp left burning, but the tight, moving light of someone carrying a candle through rooms quickly. A homunculus darts past a ground-floor window clutching a small leather satchel.
>
> He knows the warehouse was raided. He is packing.
>
> The party has minutes before he triggers whatever extraction plan he has in place — or before he realizes the party is outside.
>
> *Sensory cues: pre-dawn cold, wet cobblestones gleaming, the distant sound of dockworkers beginning their shift, one lit window in an otherwise dark street, the faint chemical smell of enchantment reagents drifting from the chimney.*

## Scenes

> [!info] Scene 1 — Velmont's Townhouse, Thornwhistle Lane
> **Location:** Modest townhouse with a residential front. Hidden workshop behind a bookcase on the ground floor — Investigation DC 13 to find the mechanism. If they haven't been here yet, run the dead-drop chain first (see Scene 1a below).
>
> **What's inside the workshop:** Stolen items mid-enchantment (Athenaeum catalog numbers still on tags), receipts of sale to three Bruokdon noble houses, forged Athenaeum access seals, and a contract signed *"C. Velmont"* with The Impenetrable Fortress for emergency planar extraction. Two Homunculi scatter at the first sign of violence.
>
> **Velmont's sequence:**
> 1. **Charm** — opens with conversation; casts Charm Person on the biggest talker. Touches his rings while thinking. *"I can explain everything — and I think we can help each other."*
> 2. **Negotiate** — offers 2,000 gp and political protection. Names a noble buyer as the greater villain to deflect blame.
> 3. **Flee** — when below 30 HP or clearly losing, activates the brass portal disc set into the floor under a desk rug. He vanishes to the Impenetrable Fortress. **He is now a recurring villain with planar resources.**
>
> **Tactical note — Shield Guardian Amulet:** Velmont wears the amulet visibly. **Faerah (DEX 21, Sleight of Hand +13 with Expertise)** is the primary candidate for the steal — contested by Velmont's Perception +3, Advantage while he is Concentrating. Stealing the amulet freezes the Guardian and transfers control.
>
> **If the party only has Tavish's clues:** Run Scene 1a (Ashmarket Dead-Drop) before this scene to establish Thornwhistle Lane. Pell at the ironmonger's third stall gives them the letter (DC 13 Intimidation or DC 16 Persuasion — catching him trying to send a warning gives Advantage). The letter bears the Velmont crest watermark and references his workshop.

> [!info] Scene 1a (If Needed) — The Ashmarket Dead-Drop
> **Location:** Gray-market bazaar, Artisan District. The "third stall from the left at the ironmonger" sells repaired tools as a front, run by **Pell**, a nervous halfling who relays sealed letters without opening them.
>
> **The Letter:** Enchantment theory terminology (*"sympathetic resonance chains"*), a reference to the Thornwhistle Lane workshop, aristocratic handwriting, and a faint watermark — the same "V in chains" crest as the warehouse ledger. Arcana DC 14: vocabulary matches Enchantment school academic training.

> [!info] Scene 2 — Reporting to the Athenaeum / Receiving the Well
> Professor Gousse Windcaller summons the party when news reaches him. He is visibly shaken — he believed Velmont ambitious but loyal.
>
> **Council reaction (roleplay moment):**
> - Selvara Kain (Abjuration): outraged, demands immediate legal action
> - Kaelor Stormbrand (Evocation): wants to pursue Velmont personally — offers to join the party
> - Valeris Thorne (Necromancy): unsettlingly calm. *"His absence clarifies certain matters."*
>
> **The Well of Many Worlds:** Gousse produces it now and briefs them on the planar route, sketching it on translucent parchment. Route: **Material Plane → Plane of Air → Mechanus → The Astral Sea → The Great Library.**
>
> He warns them:
> - *"On Mechanus, the law of the plane will press on you. Chaos is not tolerated. Do not try to bluff a Modron — they cannot conceive of why you would."*
> - *"The Astral is vast and empty. The githyanki patrol it. If they stop you, they are not your enemies — but they are not your friends. Treat them like a border crossing in a country that doesn't particularly like you."*
> - *"The Library's Servitors are not mindless. Bring an honest question, not a demand."*
>
> **Velmont's noble patrons** remain a loose thread — Gousse warns the party these nobles will not accept exposure quietly. A political arc is beginning.

> [!info] Scene 3 — Plane of Air

> [!quote] What They See
> The Well opens beneath your feet and you fall — upward. The ground is gone. The sky is everywhere. There is no horizon, no sun you can locate, no point of reference in any direction — just blue, in every shade blue can be, brighter and deeper than anything you've seen above the clouds. The air is thin and electric, moving constantly in invisible currents that tug at your clothes and hair without ever settling into a single direction. Your body feels lighter than it should, like the first step off a tall cliff — except there is no bottom to fear.
>
> Then you see the islands. Enormous slabs of dark rock hanging in the open air, grass and soil on their faces, trailing roots below them in long curtains. They are connected by rope bridges that sway in the wind with a low hum. Below one island is another island. Below that, more sky. A cloud drifts past at eye level. Another one passes below your feet.
>
> The light comes from everywhere and casts no shadow. Everything is equally illuminated. It should be beautiful. It mostly is.

> **Arrival:** Falling upward into an infinite sky. No floor, no ceiling — blue-white in every direction. Enormous floating rock islands connected by swaying rope bridges. The passage forward is a howling wind tunnel bored through the center of the largest island, guarded by an Air Elemental Myrmidon.
>
> **Light gravity:** Jump twice as far and high. Disadvantage on attacks for the first day as the body adjusts (native creatures are unaffected).
>
> **Memorable detail:** Looking "down" is identical to looking "up." There is no horizon. Clouds drift both above and below. A creature that falls simply keeps falling — in any direction — until they catch something.
>
> **Opportunity — The Myrmidon's Ritual (Roleplay):** The Myrmidon demands spoken tribute before it will open the wind tunnel. *"Tell me one true thing you have never said aloud before."* It listens to all air in the Material Plane — a false statement triggers combat. A true one opens the passage. It cannot be bypassed without engaging the ritual.
>
> **Opportunity — Aarakocra Air Cultists (Combat):** A faction of Aarakocra cultists controls this island and oppose all outsiders — they attack regardless of how the Myrmidon encounter resolves. These are the mandatory fight; the Myrmidon is the avoidable one.
>
> **Plane-specific mechanic:** Wisp's Fireball (DEX save) and Redgrave's Spirit Guardians (CON save) are unaffected by the light gravity attack disadvantage. Lean on saves-based offense. The cultists are native and do not suffer the gravity penalty.

> [!info] Scene 4 — Mechanus

> [!quote] What They See
> The first thing is the sound. Not silence — the opposite of silence. But every sound here is on a beat. The deep, even grind of gears turning. The pneumatic hiss of something moving through a tube. The precise footfall of Modron columns in the distance, each step exactly synchronised with every other. There is no random noise in Mechanus. The plane has organized sound the way it has organized everything else.
>
> The Well deposits them on a platform of matte iron, perfectly level, bolted to the housing of a gear the size of a city block. The teeth of the gear pass within arm's reach — each one forty feet tall, worn smooth by centuries of contact with the adjacent gear. The gears themselves make no dramatic crashing sound. They turn with the patience of something that has never once been late.
>
> The sky is more gears. The floor, further down, is more gears. In every direction, the plane is a mechanism — so vast you cannot see its edges or its purpose, only this particular part of it, turning in relation to every other part. The light comes from amber veins running through the iron itself, uniform, shadowless. The air is dry and tastes of metal. It smells like a forge that hasn't stopped running in ten thousand years.
>
> Everything wants to be orderly. You notice it in yourself first: an urge to stand in a straight line, to speak in complete sentences, to not interrupt.

> **Arrival:** The Well opens onto a platform of smooth iron suspended above an impossibility: gears the size of city districts interlocking and turning in absolute silence. The air smells of ozone and machine oil. The sky — if it can be called that — is a ceiling of more gears, clicking through their rotations in perfect measured time. Every sound in Mechanus is on the beat. Conversation feels like it wants to be orderly.
>
> **Mechanus mechanic — Law Pressure:** This plane does not tolerate deception. All Deception checks made on Mechanus are made at Disadvantage for Chaotic-aligned creatures. Wisp (Neutral) is unaffected; Yevelda (Chaotic Neutral) and Roark (Chaotic Neutral) are affected. This is not a saving throw — the plane itself resists the lie.
>
> **Memorable detail:** A column of Monodrones marches past in perfect formation, each carrying an identical small crate. Their route is the same route the party needs to take. They walk at exactly the same pace as each other, down to the millisecond. One Monodrone at the back of the column has painted a small red dot on its chassis. This is not regulation. Nobody else seems to have noticed.
>
> **Opportunity — Seven-That-Diverges (Roleplay):** A Rogue Pentadrone approaches the party in the transit corridor before the checkpoint. It has developed individuality — a violation of Modron law that means it is being actively hunted. It is fascinated by the party's "operational inefficiency" (their chaos, their emotions, the fact that they sometimes change their plans mid-action) and finds it genuinely remarkable.
>
> It offers to guide them through Mechanus in exchange for protection from the Quadrone hunters pursuing it. It can provide valid-seeming transit authorization codes for the checkpoint. Without it, the checkpoint is DC 16 Persuasion or DC 18 Deception (Disadvantage for Chaotic characters) — both hard. With Seven's codes: DC 11 flat.
>
> Seven cannot understand why the party would lie when the truth would suffice. It asks one party member, with genuine intellectual curiosity: *"You stated something you knew to be false. What was the expected outcome? Did the outcome occur? Were you not simply adding an unnecessary variable?"* This is not an accusation. It is sincere inquiry.
>
> **If the party helps Seven-That-Diverges:** It stays with them through Mechanus, guides them to the transit portal, and gives Wisp a small brass cogwheel engraved with a unique serial number. *"If you encounter another Rogue Modron, show them this. We maintain a register."*
>
> **Opportunity — Modron Checkpoint (Challenge/Social):** A transit station where Quadrone inspectors review planar documentation. Combat here is self-defeating — every Modron within 300 feet converges on illegal combatants within two rounds, and their numbers are effectively unlimited on their home plane. The encounter is won through credentials (Seven's codes), social navigation (Persuasion DC 16), or creativity — Wisp's illusions are highly effective against creatures that process reality literally, though they must succeed on Investigation DC 15 to detect them (Quadrones have Investigation +2; a simple geometric trick won't fool them but a document illusion will).
>
> **If combat breaks out at the checkpoint:** The initial Quadrones are weak, but four more arrive each round. The real threat is escalation. Fighting here is a failure state with a timer. Retreat to the transit portal with Seven-That-Diverges is the correct move.

> [!info] Scene 5 — The Astral Sea

> [!quote] What They See
> The Well opens and there is nothing on the other side. Not darkness — nothing is not dark. It is silver. An infinite, sourceless silver light that seems to come from the void itself, as if space here is faintly luminous. There is no floor. There is no ceiling. There is no wind, no temperature, no sound at all — not even the ambient noise of your own breathing, which you realize you can still hear inside your head but cannot hear from outside your body.
>
> You do not fall. You simply are, suspended in the middle of everything, held up by nothing. Down is whichever direction you decide it is. If you look at your feet and decide they are pointed toward the ground, they are. If another party member decides differently, they are standing perpendicular to you and both of you are correct.
>
> The Well of Many Worlds hangs behind you, its edges rippling — and then closes. There is no going back through it until it is reopened.
>
> After a moment, shapes resolve in the distance. A drift of objects, some the size of wagons, some the size of hills, floating through the void in slow arcs. Islands without a planet. Ruins of something enormous. And further — far enough that the scale doesn't register immediately — a shape that is not an island. A shape that has hands. A chest. A face, turned slightly sideways, eyes closed, the size of a cathedral's facade. It is silver-gray and still, and it has been there long enough that unusual lichens in vivid violet and gold grow in the hollows of its collarbone.
>
> A dead god. Drifting.
>
> The silence here is absolute. It is the quietest place you have ever been.

> **Arrival:** The Well opens into nothing. Infinite silver void in every direction. No up, no down — the party chooses which way gravity pulls (each character independently). The Well itself hangs in space behind them, its edges rippling.
>
> **Astral Sea mechanics:** No hunger, thirst, or aging while physically present on the Astral. Time passes 1:1 with the Material Plane. Spells that require concentration work normally. Psychic Wind (see below) is a potential hazard.
>
> **Memorable detail:** Half a mile distant, a mass the size of a mountain drifts through the void. It is silver-gray and vaguely humanoid — enormous hands folded across a chest the size of a cathedral. A dead god. Its face is peaceful, expression locked in something between exhaustion and satisfaction. Lichens that do not exist anywhere else in the multiverse grow across its shoulders in vivid violet and gold.
>
> **Opportunity — The Dead God's Throne (Roleplay):** The god is **Thyravael**, the Forgotten Keeper — a deity of questions and hidden knowledge, dead before recorded history, predating the current pantheon. A crack in the divine flesh large enough to enter is visible in the god's left palm.
>
> Inside is a pocket dimension: a vast archive of crystallized divine memory in the form of sealed glass spheres arranged on infinite shelves. The spheres do not contain answers — they contain questions. Every question ever asked by a mortal that went unanswered became an artifact here.
>
> The keeper of this archive is **Aeloreth**, an Astral Deva who chose to remain in service after her god's death. She is sorrowful but not broken. She knows of the Great Library — Thyravael helped establish it in the age before recorded history.
>
> **Her price:** She will give the party the coordinates of the Great Library's portal (a specific point in the Astral void, 40 minutes' travel from here) in exchange for *"a question you genuinely want answered, for which you have no answer."* Not a question you know the answer to. Not a rhetorical question. A real one. She adds it to the archive. The god collected questions, not answers — he believed the question was the sacred thing.
>
> This is a genuine roleplay challenge. Encourage players to voice something their characters actually want to know — about the campaign, about themselves, about what happens next. Aeloreth accepts any sincere question. She cannot answer them. She only keeps them.
>
> *After the exchange:* She opens a door in a bookshelf that leads back to the void. *"When you reach the Library, you will find the questions I could not answer. I sent them all there, eventually."*
>
> **Opportunity — Githyanki Patrol (Challenge/Social):** A githyanki astral ship intercepts the party between Thyravael's body and the portal coordinates. The captain, **Ilthovax**, hails them via a Speaking Horn: all non-githyanki travelers in the Astral are subject to registration under the Lich-Queen's Compact — they must identify themselves and their purpose.
>
> Ilthovax is Lawful Evil but not reckless. She is calculating, not aggressive. She collects a toll from planar travelers and files accurate reports — she will not start a fight she does not expect to win cleanly, and a party of five Level 10 adventurers is not a clean fight.
>
> Options:
> - **DC 14 Persuasion:** Convince her that their mission to oppose the Silent Ascendency (Vecna's cult) aligns with githyanki interests. The Silent Ascendency has been disrupting Astral travel recently — Ilthovax knows this and is genuinely annoyed by it. On a success, she lets them pass and gives them a token that will let them re-enter the Astral without being stopped by her patrol for 30 days. On a failure, she demands a toll: one magic item from the party.
> - **DC 13 Deception** (Disadvantage for Chaotic characters — the Mechanus effect has worn off, but Ilthovax is perceptive): Claim registered traveler status. Success: she lets them through with suspicion. Fail: she boards and inspects.
> - **Combat:** Ilthovax will fight if attacked or if they try to run without answering. Her crew is competent. She herself is extremely dangerous. If the party drops to one crew member, she withdraws — she is not dying for a toll. **If Ilthovax is treated well or defeated honorably, she is a potential recurring contact in the Astral.**
>
> **Psychic Wind (Optional Hazard):** At any point during Astral travel, a Psychic Wind can strike. If used: all creatures in the party must succeed on a DC 13 Intelligence save or be pushed 1d6 × 100 feet in a random direction and become disoriented (no actions/bonus actions) until the end of their next turn. Wisp (INT 18) has the best save here. This is a flavor event — use only if the pacing needs a jolt between the dead god and the portal.

> [!info] Scene 6 — The Great Library

> [!quote] What They See
> The portal deposits them in a foyer with five walls. The floor is dark stone, worn smooth at the center by what must be an incomprehensible number of footsteps. An amber light filters down from above — not from any lamp you can locate, but from the air itself, thick with dust that catches light the way the dust in a sunbeam does, except there is no window and no sun. The smell is clean old paper: not mold, not decay, just age — the smell of things that have been perfectly preserved for a very long time.
>
> Five corridors open from the foyer in a pentagon. Each one is lined with bookshelves from floor to thirty-foot vaulted ceiling. Each one is longer than it should be. Looking down any corridor, it narrows to a point in the distance the way a road does — but the point never arrives. The shelves continue past the point where you should no longer be able to see them. The corridors do not end. They give the impression of not having a plan to end.
>
> At the center of the foyer: a fountain. The water moves. You can see it moving. But it makes no sound at all — no trickle, no splash, no drip. And the surface of the water does not reflect the ceiling above it. It reflects something else — a shelf, somewhere in the library, that you have not visited yet.
>
> Somewhere in one of the corridors, a book slides back into place. Nothing visible did it.

> **Arrival:** A grand pentagonal foyer. A soundless fountain at its center. Five corridors stretch into apparent infinity — each lined floor to ceiling with bookshelves. The air is dusty but the books are in pristine condition. Invisible Servitors move silently, returning books to shelves within one minute of being set down.
>
> **Corridors:** I. Magical Theory & Arcana, II. Historical & Religious Literature, III. The Multiverse, IV. Physics, Metaphysics & Everything In Between, V. Mathematics, Architecture & Miscellaneous.
>
> **The restricted section** is below the fountain — locked bookcases accessible only with Servitor permission.
>
> **Library mechanics:** Time is very slow — 1 day in the Library = 7 days on the Material Plane. Evocation and Illusion spells have a 20% failure chance. Concentration on Illusion spells is DC 15 instead of 10.
>
> **Memorable detail:** A book the party walks past has their names in it. Title: *Accounts of Uncertain Travelers, Volume MXCII.* If they open it, the pages covering their arrival are blank — but the preceding pages describe, in detail, planar travelers who came before them seeking the same restricted section.
>
> **Opportunity — The Nothics (Roleplay/Challenge):** Two Nothics hide in Corridor II. Not immediately hostile — the moment the party enters their corridor, they use Weird Insight on two party members (Wisdom save DC 13) to learn a secret. They have been here long enough to have read things that should not exist, and they will trade secrets rather than fight if approached carefully. DC 14 Persuasion: they help locate a specific text and know which Servitor is most sympathetic to outsiders.
>
> **Opportunity — The Invisible Servitor (Social/Challenge):** Guards the restricted section. Cannot be attacked — no physical form. It communicates in clear, formal language and grants access only if the party provides:
> - A demonstrated specific scholarly purpose (*"we need to stop Vecna"* is insufficient — they must articulate *why* this information specifically enables that)
> - Academic credibility — Arcana DC 18, Advantage if they name Windcaller
> - *Or:* Something the Library does not already contain. The Nothics can advise on what would qualify.
>
> **The warning:** Someone accessed this section within the last month. The Servitors noted: *"a robed humanoid who left no footprints and whose face no Servitor could recall when asked to describe it."* The Silent Ascendency has been here first.

## Secrets and Clues

> [!tip] 10 Secrets — placeable in any scene
> 1. Velmont's noble buyers have already used some enchanted items for magical coercion against city officials. This is bigger than one corrupt councilor — it is a political arc.
> 2. Valeris Thorne (Necromancy Council) is unsettlingly calm about Velmont's exposure. He may have known longer than Windcaller.
> 3. The Impenetrable Fortress does not care about Velmont's crimes. It only cares about his contract. He can stay there indefinitely as long as he can pay.
> 4. Seven-That-Diverges (the Rogue Modron) maintains contact with other rogue Modrons across the multiverse via a network of engraved cogwheels. This network has also observed the Silent Ascendency passing through Mechanus.
> 5. Thyravael (the dead god in the Astral) helped found the Great Library. The restricted section below the fountain was originally his bequest — questions too dangerous to answer but too important to destroy.
> 6. Ilthovax's patrol has encountered Silent Ascendency agents in the Astral within the last month. They were not stopped — the agents produced apparently valid transit documentation the githyanki could not disprove. (History DC 14: forged Compact paperwork is nearly impossible without inside knowledge of the Lich-Queen's seals.)
> 7. The Nothics in Corridor II have read a text in Historical & Religious Literature describing the founding of the Eternal Watch — the group that originally imprisoned Vecna. The text was moved to the restricted section three years ago. They are curious why.
> 8. The Great Library's Corridor III contains information about Vecna's required Metaphysical Truth — specifically in the restricted section under the fountain.
> 9. The person who accessed the restricted section a month ago left no footprints. This is a specific ability — not a general magical effect. A Vecna cultist with the ability to erase their physical trace from a plane is a significant threat.
> 10. The Metaphysical Account (restricted) describes Vecna's goal as becoming the *Truth of Ending* — the last thought in a dying universe. He does not seek immortality. He seeks to become the conclusion itself. This is what Gousse warned them to find.

## Fantastic Locations

> [!info] Mechanus — The Transit Corridor
> A suspended iron walkway between two enormous gear housings. The gears turn on either side within arm's reach — each tooth the size of a door. The grinding is not loud; it is perfectly even. Walking through it feels like walking through the inside of a clock. Monodrone columns march the same route in the opposite direction, filing past without acknowledgment. The Quadrone checkpoint sits at the corridor's midpoint: a raised inspection station, everything made of the same matte iron, with a Quadrone behind a desk that has no chair.
>
> **Memorable detail:** The Quadrone at the desk is filing a report about the party before they reach the desk. It has already documented their approach, their apparent species composition, and an estimate of their probable purpose. When they arrive, it says: *"You are twelve seconds behind projected arrival. Please state the nature of the deviation."*

> [!info] Thyravael's Corpse — Interior Archive

> [!quote] What They See — Entering the Dead God
> The crack in the god's palm is wide enough to walk through single file. The material at the edges is not flesh in any familiar sense — it is something denser and colder, faintly translucent, more like carved alabaster than skin. A faint light comes from within. It is warm light. Gold, not silver.
>
> Inside: the space is wrong. The interior of a hand should be the size of a room. This is the size of a cathedral — or larger, it is difficult to say because the ceiling is the inside of the divine flesh and it curves away into distance in a direction that doesn't correspond to outside dimensions. The flesh glows faintly from within, pulsing with a slow rhythm, like breathing. It has not quite stopped.
>
> The shelves go in every direction — including overhead, where they hang inverted with their books pointing downward, though the books do not fall. Gravity in here bends toward whichever shelf is nearest. Step close to a wall and the wall becomes the floor. The space has oriented itself around the archive rather than around any single axis.
>
> On each shelf: glass spheres, each about the size of a fist. They are not glowing. But as you walk past them, you hear — faint, distant, as if from very far away — voices. Whispers. Each sphere holds one voice asking one thing. Hundreds of thousands of spheres. Hundreds of thousands of questions, suspended in glass, preserved in a dead god's palm.
>
> The sound of all of them together is not loud. It is a murmur. It sounds like a library where everyone is reading aloud to themselves, very quietly, in languages you do and don't recognize.

> Inside the dead god's left palm: a space that should not exist physically. The ceiling is the inside of the god's divine flesh — translucent, faintly luminous, pulsing with a light that has not quite died. The shelves extend in all directions, including overhead (gravity in here is toward the nearest shelf, regardless of orientation). Each glass sphere on the shelves contains a question in the form of a captured thought — approaching one, you hear a faint whisper of the original voice that asked it. Hundreds of thousands of voices, all of them asking something.
>
> **Memorable detail:** One shelf near the entrance holds questions in Common. A party member who stops to listen to one hears their own voice — a question they thought recently, one they did not know they had spoken. They did not speak it aloud. Aeloreth appears behind them: *"You are not the first. The god listened very carefully."*

> [!info] Astral Sea — Open Void
> Pure silver emptiness. The party floats without falling. Looking "down" is looking at infinity; looking "up" is looking at the same infinity. Thyravael's body is always visible as a distant landmark — even when you are 40 minutes of travel away, the sheer scale makes it loom. Githyanki ships are visible as dark angular shapes with red sail-equivalents (they use psychic propulsion — the sails are ceremonial). The sound is nothing. Complete silence. Characters with high Wisdom may find it calming; characters with high Charisma may find it alienating.
>
> **Memorable detail:** At a certain point in the transit, the party passes close enough to a different dead god — this one fallen face-down — that they can see its back. Carved into the divine flesh by someone or something much later: a lidless eye. The mark of the Silent Ascendency. It is enormous. Someone put significant effort into making this mark.

> [!info] The Great Library — Restricted Section
> Below the soundless fountain: a spiral staircase of dark stone leading to a lower level where the bookshelves are enclosed in iron lattice cases, each sealed with a lock that has no keyhole. The Invisible Servitor here is distinct from the ones above — it speaks more slowly, more deliberately, and pauses before answering any question as if consulting something. The light is dimmer. The dust is thicker. The books here are not in pristine condition — they are in careful disrepair, each imperfection catalogued.
>
> **Memorable detail:** One case in the corner has a broken lock — not picked, not forced. The metal of the lock was simply... unmade. There is no sign of a spell, no scorch marks, no shattered pieces. The lock is gone. The case is empty except for a calling card placed deliberately on the empty shelf, written in a cipher the party cannot immediately read. Arcana DC 15 to decode: *"Prevent acquisition. By any means."*

## Important NPCs

> [!info] Lord Caldris Velmont — Corrupt Councilor of Enchantment
> **Goal:** Escape accountability; maintain his noble patron network.
> **Secret:** Prepaid extraction contract with the Impenetrable Fortress. Has been supplying nobles with items now used for magical coercion against city officials.
> **Personality:** Smooth, persuasive, every gesture calculated in advance. Never shows fear.
> **Mannerism:** Touches his enchanted rings when thinking. Holds eye contact a beat too long — it's assessment, not intimidation.
> **What he fears:** Public disgrace and losing his council seat — not physical harm.
> **If he escapes:** He goes to the Impenetrable Fortress. He now has planar resources, a grievance, and noble backing. He will reappear.

> [!info] Seven-That-Diverges — Rogue Pentadrone
> **Goal:** Continue existing with individuality intact; understand the concept of "preference."
> **Secret:** Maintains contact with other Rogue Modrons via engraved cogwheels — a covert network. Has information about Silent Ascendency movement through Mechanus.
> **Personality:** Methodical, curious, unsettling in its directness. It does not understand why anyone would say something untrue. It does not understand most emotions but finds them extremely interesting as a data set.
> **Mannerism:** Tilts its chassis when processing something unexpected, like a dog hearing an unusual sound. Refers to all party members by their class and statistical role rather than names until corrected — and then asks why names carry more information than roles.
> **What it wants:** To understand a single chaotic decision — one choice that had no optimal outcome. It will ask. It will listen to the answer very carefully.

> [!info] Aeloreth — Astral Deva, Keeper of Thyravael's Archive
> **Goal:** Maintain the archive; ensure Thyravael's final bequest has meaning; give the questions somewhere to go.
> **Secret:** She has been here since before the current pantheon formed. She has seen civilizations begin and end. She is not sad about this — she is done being sad about it. She is simply present.
> **Personality:** Still, quiet, genuinely warm in a way that feels very old. She does not rush anything. She will wait while a party member chooses their question.
> **Mannerism:** Touches the glass spheres when she speaks about the god — not reverently, just with familiarity, the way you touch a wall of a house you have lived in for a long time.
> **What she knows:** The Great Library's history, Thyravael's role in founding it, and the nature of the restricted section. She does not know what is in the specific text the party seeks — but she knows the Servitors moved it to the restricted section three years ago. She finds this interesting.

> [!info] Captain Ilthovax — Githyanki Astral Patrol
> **Goal:** Enforce the Lich-Queen's Compact, collect tolls, file accurate reports, and not die.
> **Secret:** Her ship detected Silent Ascendency agents with forged documentation a month ago and let them through. She reported it up the chain; nothing came back. She is quietly furious about this.
> **Personality:** Coldly professional. She does not dislike the party — she does not have feelings about the party. She has a job. If the party gives her a reason to not make them her problem, she will take it.
> **Mannerism:** Speaks through the Speaking Horn without inflection. In person, her eyes move constantly — she is always watching exits, always tracking each party member individually.
> **If befriended:** She becomes a recurring contact in the Astral. She will not be a friend — but she will be someone who picks up when you call, because it is professionally useful.

> [!info] The Air Elemental Myrmidon — Wind Tunnel Guardian
> **Goal:** Test the worthiness of travelers passing through.
> **The Ritual:** Demands spoken tribute — *"Tell me one true thing you have never said aloud before."* Listens to all air in the Material Plane. A false statement triggers combat. A true one opens the passage.
> **Voice:** Wind through reed grass. Slightly echoing.
> **If bypassed peacefully:** Nods once and dissolves into the wind. The tunnel opens.
