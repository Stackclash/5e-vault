## Strong Start

> [!info] Picking Up Exactly Where S42 Left Off
> Cross-cut cold open — no recap narration, just drop back into the freeze-frame from last session and let it move.
>
> *At the Umbral Athenaeum, Professor Gousse's quill has stopped moving. Redgrave's optical sensors, calibrated to notice more than they let on, register something small and wrong about the man across the desk — a held stillness that reads less like calm and more like a performance of it.*
>
> *Three streets away, in a basement that shouldn't exist, four of you are looking at Lord Caldris Velmont. He is looking back. He has been waiting.*

## Scenes

> [!info] Scene 1 — The Athenaeum Office (Redgrave & Gousse)
> **Who's really across the desk:** a doppelganger, codenamed **Marrow** by its Silent Ascendency handlers, wearing Velmont's face. It's been running his public life for weeks — this scene, and the vault scene below, run concurrently. Cut between them; don't resolve one before starting the other.
>
> **Playing Marrow:** smooth, a little too smooth — it's had weeks to study Velmont's mannerisms via Read Thoughts but doesn't have his actual judgment, so it over-performs the calculated calm rather than naturally having it. It's here to find out what the party knows and stall.
>
> **The tell:** Marrow cannot cast any spell, use any magic item, or produce any magical effect — a doppelganger's only tools are Shapechanger, Read Thoughts, and two Slam attacks. Any situation that would call for Velmont to do something arcane (Identify an item, cast a minor spell, even just touch his rings the way he habitually does while thinking) exposes it immediately. A Detect Magic or Arcana check on "him" reads oddly inert — his rings hold no charge at all.
>
> **If it's made:** Marrow doesn't fight to the death — CR 3, squishy next to a warforged envoy. It shapechanges into someone else in the room (a clerk, a passing scholar) and tries to slip out rather than brawl. Capturing it alive is a real intelligence win — a live, named source on Ascendency infiltration methods. If it escapes, it's a recurring threat that now knows the party's faces.
>
> **If it isn't made quickly:** it fishes for what the party found at the townhouse, tries to redirect suspicion toward "corrupt subordinates," and looks for an excuse to end the meeting and disappear.

> [!info] Scene 2 — The Hidden Vault (Wisp, Roark, Yevelda, Faerah)
> **The room:** Velmont's own panic room, built and warded by him personally — antimagic in nature, but permeable to Enchantment specifically (his own school). This is why he was never able to escape it once the tables turned, and why the Ascendency's caster used this exact room to brand him: it's the one place in the house their Enchantment magic could reliably reach him.
>
> **Layout:** a cramped stone chamber below the townhouse, reached by a narrow stair (chokepoint on entry — don't let the party spread out before the golem gets a look at them). Low ceiling, poor maneuvering room for a Large creature and four PCs both. Velmont is restrained across the room, out of easy reach of the golem's 5 ft attacks.
>
> **The golem:** already Velmont's own long-standing vault guardian, not something the Ascendency built — golems can't be built quickly, and this network hasn't had the four days since Vecna's escape to commission one. Marrow simply learned Velmont's command words via Read Thoughts and re-tasked its standing orders: contain the prisoner, destroy intruders. It attacks the moment the party is noticed; it can't be reasoned with.
>
> **The lockout (the real difficulty engine, not raw stats):** the ward suppresses spellcasting and magic items exactly like a normal Antimagic Field — it just carves out an exception for Enchantment-school spells specifically. That exception does **not** extend to magic weapons or items (items aren't school-tagged; they're their own suppressed category under Antimagic Field). Practical result: **nonmagical weapons, and magic weapons whose properties are currently suppressed, do zero damage to the golem** (immune to bludgeoning/piercing/slashing from nonmagical attacks unless adamantine). Wisp's spells are dead here too (Illusion, not Enchantment). Telegraph this on the very first swing — a blade "sparks harmlessly off the stone, as if it forgot what it was" — so the table pivots to the real objective instead of flailing blind.
>
> **The anchor:** a warded stone/medallion is the ward's physical source, somewhere in the chamber (floor medallion, carved pillar). Investigation DC 13–15 to spot it (seed an early clue — amber-free plain stone veining that all traces back to one point — so it's findable within a round or two of active searching). To disable: DC 16 Arcana (undoing the ward properly) or DC 15 Thieves' Tools (physically defeating the lock), or DC 18 Strength to just shatter it — loud, and a good noise cue into Scene 1 if you want the two scenes to touch (Marrow gets jumpy upstairs). Disabling it drops the ward entirely: magic weapons wake up, golem goes down fast (2–3 rounds with full party output).
>
> **Velmont, throughout — not just before or after:** his Feeblemind-style failsafe (Ascendency-placed, not his own) is live the **entire time**, ward up or down — Enchantment passes through his own ward regardless of its state, so there's no safe window, ever. He knows it's there and self-censors constantly: circling answers, gesturing, writing instead of saying the dangerous thing outright. He's still useful — the anchor's location and (carefully) his account of what happened to him are things he can say without triggering it — but extracting anything requires the party to ask around the edges, not head-on.
>
> **The fork, once the golem's down:** for the first time since this started, Velmont isn't guarded or chained by the ward. He is Neutral Evil and does not want to become anyone's asset — his instinct is to slip away and deal with his own curse later, on his own terms. Don't script this: let the party's handling of the moment (persuasion, a grab, simply not noticing in time) decide whether he stays. If he runs, he's a live thread, not a lost one — cursed, exposed, and now moving.
>
> **The cure, if they keep him and want to try it (same session, no separate quest):** he can describe the ritual's *mechanism* without tripping the failsafe (that's not the forbidden content) — enough of a lead combined with physical evidence still in the room (scorch marks from the casting circle, a dropped page of notation, spent reagents — Investigation DC 13 to notice, Arcana DC 15 to partially read) to reconstruct the counter. A hidden cache elsewhere in the room (false panel, Perception/Investigation DC 13; DC 13 Thieves' Tools or Strength to open) holds the matching counter-reagents. Combining testimony + residue + reagents into a working ritual: Arcana DC 16 (advantage if they have all three pieces). This doesn't need a caster with Enchantment prepared — frame it as procedural/ritual, achievable by anyone competent, since none of the four present know Enchantment magic and Gousse isn't at this location to help. Standard Greater Restoration/Heal do **not** work on this — it's a bespoke Ascendency curse, not the PHB spell, and Velmont himself doesn't know a full cure since the Ascendency built it, not him.

> [!info] Scene 3 — Convergence
> The two groups reunite after real travel time — different locations across the city, not a hand-off in the same building. What they bring to the table depends entirely on how Scenes 1–2 landed:
> - Marrow captured, escaped, or dead?
> - Velmont cured-and-cooperative, fled, or in custody uncured?
>
> This is where the session's actual takeaway crystallizes. If Velmont's an asset: he can offer his ongoing smuggling deliveries as a channel to trace the Ascendency's supply chain upward — a natural close to Hunt Stage 2 (Harvest Power) and a lead into Stage 3. Hold this as a DM-only lever either way: he'll sell the party out again the instant it profits him more.

> [!info] Scene 4 — Stretch Goal (Optional)
> The Well of Many Worlds / planar journey to the Great Library (Plane of Air → Mechanus → Astral Sea → Great Library) is already fully built in the prior session prep and never got used. Don't force it into tonight — Scenes 1–3 are dense enough on their own. Only reach for this if the table clears everything above with time to spare.

## Secrets and Clues

> [!tip] 10 Secrets — placeable in any scene
> 1. "Velmont" at the Athenaeum can't perform any magic at all — a doppelganger's only tools are its slam attacks, Read Thoughts, and a stolen face.
> 2. The real Velmont has been held in his own vault for weeks — kept alive because Marrow needs him nearby to refresh the impersonation via Read Thoughts.
> 3. The antimagic ward is Velmont's own creation, built to shield his smuggling secrets from Athenaeum surveillance — deliberately permeable to Enchantment, his own specialty.
> 4. The Silent Ascendency predates Vecna's escape by a good while — what's new in the last four days is the *escalation* (targeting the party's own power directly), not the network's existence.
> 5. Velmont's failsafe is Ascendency-made, custom, and doesn't respond to Greater Restoration or Heal — and it's permanently active regardless of the ward's state.
> 6. Tavish's identical brand (S42) came from the same apparatus — a pattern of control over compromised assets, not a one-off.
> 7. Physical residue in the vault (scorch marks, a dropped page, spent reagents) shows the Ascendency's caster used this exact room to brand him — the one place in the house their Enchantment magic would reliably work.
> 8. The golem is Velmont's own long-standing guardian, not an Ascendency asset — Marrow simply learned his command words and re-tasked it.
> 9. Freed from the golem's guard, Velmont's first instinct is self-preservation, not gratitude.
> 10. The excess volume moving through Velmont's smuggling ring — far more than his own operation would ever need — is the Ascendency siphoning material toward the ascension, likely without Velmont ever fully clocking it himself.

## Important NPCs

> [!info] Marrow — Doppelganger, Silent Ascendency Infiltrator
> **Goal:** maintain the Velmont identity, learn what the party knows, avoid exposure.
> **Secret:** has been running Velmont's public life for weeks by studying him via Read Thoughts; reports up an Ascendency chain the party hasn't identified yet.
> **Personality:** performs Velmont's calculated calm a half-step too perfectly — smooth in a way that reads as rehearsed even to those who don't know why.
> **Mannerism:** doesn't touch his rings the way the real Velmont does (they hold no charge) — the tell, if anyone thinks to check.
> **What it fears:** exposure before it can report back or escape.
> **If it escapes:** recurring shapeshifted threat, now aware of the party's faces and at least some of what they know.

> [!info] Lord Caldris Velmont — the Real One
> **Current state:** restrained in his own vault, Feeblemind failsafe permanently live, self-censoring on instinct after weeks of practice.
> **Goal (this scene):** survive, get free, and — if the party proves useful rather than dangerous — leverage them for protection and vengeance against Marrow. Not loyalty. Utility.
> **Secret:** knows less about the Ascendency's actual structure than the party might hope — he's been a prisoner, not a briefed operative.
> **Personality/mannerism:** unchanged from his NPC sheet — smooth, calculating, rehearsed-feeling — just redirected at survival instead of diplomacy. Still touches his rings when thinking, out of habit, even shackled.
> **What he fears:** being made someone's pawn permanently. If he sees a clean exit once the golem's down, he'll take it rather than negotiate.

> [!info] Professor Gousse Windcaller
> Present at the Athenaeum for Scene 1 — genuinely fond of "Velmont" and slow to suspect a colleague of years, which is exactly why Marrow chose this cover. If the doppelganger is exposed in front of him, play his shock as real; this cuts close to his own judgment.

## Potential Monsters

> [!warning] Scene 1 — Marrow (Discovery-Weighted, Not a Hard Fight)
> #### 1× Doppelganger — *[[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Monstrosity/Doppelganger.md|Doppelganger]], CR 3 (MM p.82)*
> AC 14, HP 52. Multiattack: two Slams (+6, 1d6+4 each). Ambusher/Surprise Attack if it gets a jump on someone. Read Thoughts gives it advantage on Insight/Deception/Intimidation/Persuasion against whoever it's reading. Condition-immune to charmed. No spellcasting whatsoever — that absence is the whole point of this encounter. Redgrave alone likely outclasses it if it comes to blows; the scene's tension is discovery and containment, not survivability.

> [!warning] Scene 2 — The Golem (Hard, via Lockout Not Raw Stats)
> #### 1× Stone Golem — *[[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Construct/Stone Golem.md|Stone Golem]], CR 10 (MM p.170)*
> AC 17, HP 178. Multiattack: two Slams (+10, 3d8+6 each). Slow (Recharge 5–6): DC 17 WIS or lose reactions, halve speed, one action-type only, 1 minute (repeatable save each turn). Immune to poison, psychic, and nonmagical bludgeoning/piercing/slashing unless adamantine. Immune to charmed, exhaustion, frightened, paralyzed, petrified, poisoned. No legendary actions — the difficulty here comes entirely from the antimagic lockout on weapon damage, not the stat block. Party is only 4 of 5 PCs (no Redgrave); Rage, Sneak Attack, and other class features (not spells) function normally throughout regardless of the ward.
>
> **XP math (Lazy DM benchmark, per the party note's default):** 4 characters × level 10 = 40 total levels; "potentially deadly" at CR sum > 20 (half, since 5th level+). A lone CR 10 golem (CR sum 10) reads as safe by that heuristic alone — the antimagic/damage-immunity interaction is what actually makes this hard, not the raw CR. Don't add a second combatant to compensate; use the anchor puzzle to split the party's actions instead (Wisp searching while others hold the line).

## Potential Treasure

> [!info] Velmont's Vault
> - **Evidence of the Ascendency's smuggling volume** — ledgers showing purchases far beyond what Velmont's own ring would ever need, in Marrow's handwriting rather than Velmont's — a forensic tell in itself if compared against his real signature.
> - **The counter-reagents** (used for the cure, not tradeable) — a set of clay/crystal/glass spheres, deliberately keyed differently from a standard Feeblemind's material component.
> - **A ledger or contact fragment** pointing toward whoever in the Ascendency placed the brand — the next thread if the party wants to pull on it.
> - Mundane coin and valuables at your discretion — this room was a hiding place, not a treasure vault.
