## Strong Start

> [!info] The Athenaeum, Two Days After S43
> Cold open on Gousse's office — no recap needed if S43 landed clean; if it didn't, one line covers it: whatever happened with Velmont/Marrow, the Ascendency thread is quiet for the moment. Gousse has been waiting.
>
> *Gousse doesn't stand when you enter. He's surrounded by open charts — not maps of Bruokdon, or even Eldoria. Concentric circles, annotated in a hand that isn't his, borrowed from older Athenaeum survey records. He looks up like a man who has finally solved a problem he's been chewing on for weeks.*

> [!quote] Gousse Windcaller
> "I've been thinking about the Well wrong. We both have. It doesn't open onto a plane at random, and it doesn't open onto whichever plane you'd *like*. It answers resonance. Open it somewhere the wind never stops, and it hears the Plane of Air. Open it in fire, it hears fire. You are not commanding the Well — you are *agreeing* with it about where you already are. Choose your ground badly, and it simply won't open at all."
>
> *He taps three marked sites on the chart in turn.*
>
> "I've found three sites. A storm coast the old records blame for a century of missing ships. A fey ring no one's surveyed because no one who's gone looking has come back with a straight answer about what they found. And a quarantined yard the Athenaeum has warned students off for longer than I've been alive — officially a magical hazard. I don't think that's all it is anymore."
>
> "Three crossings. Then the Library."

Let the party ask questions, requisition anything reasonable, and choose their own pacing before stepping through. Cut to Scene 1 the moment they commit.

## Scenes

> [!info] Scene 1 — The Plane of Water

> [!quote] What They See
> The Well opens and the storm swallows you whole — except there's no surface to hit. You're already under. Sunlight filters down in shifting gold ropes from somewhere that might be up, and below that, the gold gives out entirely into a blue so dark it reads as black. The water doesn't feel like water so much as a place with its own weather: warm currents and cold currents crossing in visible seams, distant thunder that arrives as pressure in your chest rather than sound. Coral spires rise around you like a drowned forest, some the size of houses, hung with things that might be gardens.
>
> Far below, past the point your eyes give up, something changes the quality of the dark. It isn't coming toward you. Not yet.

> **Arrival:** The party surfaces mid-transition between the **Sea of Light** (sunlit, coral, inhabited) and the **Darkened Depths** (lightless, crushing). No fixed "up" — only "toward the gold" or "toward the black."
>
> **The clock:** Every minute spent without reaching the resonant anchor (a coral spire/atoll), each character makes a **DC 12 Constitution save** (DC +1 each subsequent check) **or gains a level of exhaustion**. A tension clock, not a trap — it punishes dawdling, not failed rolls.
>
> **Reaching the anchor (splittable tasks):**
> - **Way-finding:** DC 13 Survival or Perception to read the current toward the Sea of Light. Failure just burns a minute, no harm.
> - **Swimming it:** DC 14 Strength (Athletics) against the current — automatic for anyone with a swim speed or Water Breathing active. Help action works normally.
>
> **Opportunity — Coralkin Scouts (friendly, optional):** Aquatic scouts loyal to the marids investigate the "sky-fallen" travelers. Curious, not hostile. They'll guide the party straight to the anchor — skipping the clock entirely — for something genuinely novel from the surface: a story, a bit of parlor magic, a plant they've never seen. Cheap to give, good roleplay.
>
> **The thing in the dark:** Never statted, never meant to be fought. It only closes distance if the party racks up 3+ failed clock checks or throws loud, aggressive magic into the water. If it starts closing in, that's the cue to reach the anchor *now* — a chase beat, not a combat encounter. (If it ever needs real stats because the table forces the issue, reflavor [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Dragon/Dragon Turtle.md|Dragon Turtle]], CR 17 — a name that should stay firmly out of the players' hands as a threat to actually fight.)

> [!info] Scene 2 — The Feywild

> [!quote] What They See
> The Well opens onto everlasting dusk. The sun — if it is a sun — sits low and orange at the horizon and does not move while you watch it, casting long violet shadows through a wood that's too symmetrical to be wild and too alive to be planted. Faerie lights drift between the trees at knee height, in no hurry. Somewhere close, unseen, something is playing four notes on an instrument none of you can name, over and over, pleasantly, without ever quite finishing the phrase.
>
> At the center of a ring of standing stones, a figure waits. She was clearly expecting you.

> **The trap:** The figure is [[Baba Lysaga (COS)|Baba Lysaga]], wearing a fey glamour granted by [[Mab]] as part of their bargain. She plays a bargain-broker straight — courteous, a little theatrical, offering safe passage or useful knowledge for "one true thing you've never said aloud." It's a fair Feywild bargain right up until they take it.
>
> **Seeing through it early:** any character can attempt **Insight (DC 17, her spell save DC)** if something prompts suspicion (an odd word choice, a too-perfect answer). Success doesn't stop the ambush, but that character isn't surprised when it springs.
>
> **The reveal:** the instant a bargain is struck (or the party gets suspicious/aggressive enough to force it), the glamour drops. Everyone who didn't see through it is **surprised** for the first round.
>
> **The fight — tilted on purpose:** terrain is unseelie bramble, difficult terrain for the party only. Round 1 opens with Baleful Baying scattering the party into it and away from each other; Lysaga follows with battlefield control (Evard's Black Tentacles/Cloudkill) from range on whoever gets isolated, never melee. At ~⅓ HP she Shapechanges into a swarm of insects and flees — anything merely carried, not worn, is dropped. **Driving her off is a full win; the kill isn't required.** The hounds don't flee with her — on loan from Mab, they fight until the encounter resolves, then vanish back to her service either way. Full tactics and stat block reference in Potential Monsters below.
>
> **Mab arrives after:** she doesn't fight, doesn't threaten. A bargain was struck on her ground in her name without her present — that makes it hers to settle. Coolly gracious throughout; this is the party's introduction to her.

> [!quote] Mab
> "A bargain was struck on my ground, in my name, without my presence. That makes it mine to settle, whether or not she finishes what she started. You're not owed an apology. You're owed a choice. I'd like to make you one."

> **Mab's offer:** safe passage onward, no further fight, in exchange for:
> 1. **Immediate and personal** — she names and takes something specific from one PC of her choosing (a formative memory, a true name, something with real weight but not gone forever — recoverable later through a quest, a *wish*, or another bargain with her court).
> 2. **A marker, unspecified** — one future favor, on her terms, at a time of her choosing. Don't define it now; it's a hook for later, once you know what would actually cost the party something to refuse.
>
> **If refused:** she doesn't force it — she simply doesn't smooth the way onward. The next crossing gets harder to find (DM's discretion on the complication). She's transactional, not cruel, and has nothing to gain from trapping them here.

> [!info] Scene 3 — Limbo

> [!quote] What They See
> The Well opens onto nothing holding still. A stretch of what was stone a second ago runs liquid, then goes to ringing metal, then breathes out as smoke and comes down as snow that was never cold. There's no ground to stand on and no sky to fall toward — you simply hang there, and moving is a matter of deciding to. Fragments of somewhere else drift past at odd distances: a stretch of hedge, a wedge of castle wall, a stream running nowhere in particular, none of them connected to anything, none of them staying long enough to matter.
>
> About four hundred feet distant, impossibly, something is *not* moving. A shape too regular for this place — square edges, a held stillness — sits anchored against the churn like it simply refused to participate.

> **Arrival:** No gravity — movement is willed, not walked (a creature moves up to its speed in any direction just by intending it). The stable shape is a **githzerai enclave**, one of the "islands of invention" disciplined minds can hold together here.
>
> **The crossing — Power of the Mind (RAW Limbo mechanic):** Each round moving through unstabilized Limbo, a character not inside a stabilized bubble makes a **DC 13 Intelligence save** or the matter around them transforms mid-step — minor, non-lethal chaos (spun off course, briefly encased, a held item transforms and must be dropped or re-drawn). Alternatively, spend an action:
>
> > As an action, an Intelligence check to **stabilize a spherical area** centered on the creature: base DC 5 for a 10-ft. radius, +5 per additional 10 ft. Success holds the bubble for 24 hours or until reused.
>
> Play it as a leapfrogging puzzle — best INT throws the bubble, the party crosses into it, then pushes the next one forward. Rewards coordination over brute force.
>
> **Opportunity — the Githzerai Enclave (roleplay, avoidable):** [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Humanoid/Githzerai Monk.md|Githzerai Monks]] (CR 2) led by a [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Humanoid/Githzerai Zerth.md|Githzerai Zerth]] (CR 6) offer a stabilized shortcut to the next resonance point for a brief trial — not combat, composure: one representative PC must stabilize a 10-ft. bubble unassisted (DC 5, trivially easy — the trial is about doing it alone under scrutiny, not the number).
>
> **If the party is loud, careless, or slow:** chaos notices. A [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Aberration/Red Slaad.md|Red Slaad]] (CR 5) or [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Aberration/Blue Slaad.md|Blue Slaad]] (CR 7) drifts into view — dangerous solo, but under no obligation to engage anything not in its way. An avoid-or-flee beat, not a scripted fight. If the party impressed the enclave, the githzerai intervene to drive it off.

> [!info] Scene 4 — The Great Library

> [!quote] What They See
> The portal deposits them in a foyer with five walls. The floor is dark stone, worn smooth at the center by an incomprehensible number of footsteps. An amber light filters down from above — not from any lamp you can locate, but from the air itself, thick with dust that catches light the way dust in a sunbeam does, except there is no window and no sun. The smell is clean old paper: not mold, not decay, just age.
>
> Five corridors open from the foyer in a pentagon, lined floor to a thirty-foot vaulted ceiling with bookshelves that narrow toward a vanishing point that never arrives. At the center: a soundless fountain, its surface reflecting a shelf somewhere in the library the party hasn't visited yet.

> **Corridors:** I. Magical Theory & Arcana, II. Historical & Religious Literature, III. The Multiverse, IV. Physics, Metaphysics & Everything In Between, V. Mathematics, Architecture & Miscellaneous.
>
> **Library mechanics:** Time is very slow — 1 day here = 7 days on the Material Plane. Evocation and Illusion spells have a 20% failure chance; Concentration on Illusion spells is DC 15 instead of 10.
>
> **Opportunity — The Nothics (Corridor II):** Two Nothics use Weird Insight on entry (WIS DC 13) to learn a secret from two party members. Not immediately hostile — they'll trade secrets and point toward a sympathetic Servitor rather than fight if approached carefully (DC 14 Persuasion for active cooperation).
>
> **Opportunity — The Invisible Servitor:** Guards the restricted section below the fountain. Grants access for a demonstrated scholarly purpose (not "we need to stop Vecna" alone — they must articulate *why* this specifically enables that), academic credibility (Arcana DC 18, advantage if they name Windcaller), or something the Library doesn't already contain.
>
> **The warning:** someone accessed the restricted section within the last month — "a robed humanoid who left no footprints, whose face no Servitor could recall when asked to describe it." The Silent Ascendency got here first.
>
> **The payoff:** *The Metaphysical Account* — the party's first real understanding of Vecna's actual goal: not immortality, but to become the **Truth of Ending**, the last thought in a dying universe.

## Secrets and Clues

> [!tip] 10 Secrets — placeable in any scene
> 1. Gousse's resonance principle isn't new theory — it's in the borrowed survey records themselves, written in a hand that isn't his. He hasn't said whose.
> 2. The storm coast's missing-ship history spans a century — the Water-plane bleed-through has been happening long before anyone had a name for it.
> 3. Baba Lysaga's glamour was good enough to fool trained observers — Mab's fey magic, not her own; she couldn't have managed the disguise alone.
> 4. Strahd's death is barely a week old to Baba Lysaga, by her own reckoning — she has had no time to cool, only to plan.
> 5. The bargain that armed Lysaga's trap (the Yeth Hounds, the glamour) cost her a future favor to Mab — a debt she hasn't paid yet and may never get the chance to on her own terms.
> 6. Mab has never broken a bargain in her long rule of the Gloaming Court — her word is absolute, which makes her both trustworthy and dangerous to owe.
> 7. The quarantined "magical hazard" yard the Athenaeum warns students from is, and has always been, a thin seam to Limbo — the Athenaeum's official explanation was always incomplete, possibly deliberately.
> 8. The githzerai enclave in Limbo maintains its stability through discipline alone — no walls, no wards, just held intent. It has stood for longer than anyone in the party's lifetime.
> 9. The Great Library's restricted section was accessed within the last month by someone who left no trace — a specific, rare ability, not a general magical effect.
> 10. *The Metaphysical Account* confirms Vecna doesn't seek immortality — he seeks to become the Truth of Ending, the last thought in a dying universe. This is what Gousse warned them to find.

## Important NPCs

> [!info] Baba Lysaga — Vengeful, Cunning, Not a Duelist
> **Goal:** avenge Strahd's death; make Strahd's killers pay for the theft of her hut's gem.
> **Secret:** barely a week removed from the loss by her own reckoning — fresh grief, not stale rage. Bargained a future favor to Mab for the means to spring this trap.
> **Personality:** theatrical and courteous as the disguised broker; cold and precise once revealed. Fights smart, never fair.
> **If she escapes:** she remains a live, recurring threat — a debt she owes Mab still hangs over her too, and she'll need something to offer next time.

> [!info] Mab — Queen of Air and Darkness, Ruler of the Gloaming Court
> **Goal:** collect what's owed her; assess what value these particular mortals represent.
> **Personality:** coldly gracious, unhurried, never raises her voice. Never lies — never volunteers the whole truth either.
> **Mannerism:** owls and ravens settle near her unbidden and go still.
> **What she wants here:** the immediate price plus an open marker — she's playing a longer game than Lysaga's vendetta.

> [!info] The Githzerai Enclave (Limbo)
> Not a single named NPC — a small, disciplined order that judges the party by composure under chaos, not combat prowess. If impressed, a durable ally in a plane the party will very likely need to cross again.

## Potential Monsters

> [!warning] Scene 2 — Baba Lysaga's Trap (Hard, Tilted by Design)
> ```encounter
> name: Baba Lysaga's Trap
> creatures:
>   - 1: [[Baba Lysaga (COS)]]
>   - 2: [[Yeth Hound (MPMM)]]
> ```
> **CR math (Lazy DM benchmark):** 5 characters × level 10 = 50 total levels; deadly at CR sum > 25. Lysaga (11) + 2× Yeth Hound (4 each) = 19 — Hard, not Deadly, by raw CR. The real tilt is tactical: ambush surprise on the reveal, bramble terrain that only slows the party, Baleful Baying scattering the group before Lysaga's control spells (Evard's Black Tentacles, Cloudkill) isolate whoever got separated. She never melees. At ~⅓ HP (~40) she Shapechanges into a swarm of insects and flees — driving her off ends the encounter cleanly; the hounds fight on or vanish regardless of her fate.
> **Yeth Hound notes:** damage immunity to nonmagical weapons (unless silvered) — check who in the party is still swinging nonmagical steel at level 10 before running this. Baleful Baying is DC 13 Wis, 300-ft range, forces frightened targets to spend their whole move fleeing directly away.

> [!info] Scene 1 — The Thing in the Dark (Avoid, Never Statted)
> Deliberately left unstatted — its entire function is presence, not combat. If a table insists on fighting it, [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Dragon/Dragon Turtle.md|Dragon Turtle]] (CR 17) is the reference stat block, but running it as a real fight is a last resort, not the intended path.

> [!info] Scene 3 — Slaad Wildcard (Avoidable)
> #### 1× [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Aberration/Red Slaad.md|Red Slaad]], CR 5, or [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Aberration/Blue Slaad.md|Blue Slaad]], CR 7
> Only appears if the party is loud, slow, or careless crossing the unstabilized stretch. Not hunting the party specifically — has no reason to engage anything not directly in its way. The githzerai enclave will intervene on the party's behalf if the trial went well.

> [!info] Scene 4 — The Nothics (Non-Combat Preferred)
> #### 2× [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Aberration/Nothic.md|Nothic]], CR 2 each
> Weird Insight (WIS DC 13) on entry to Corridor II. Trade secrets rather than fight if approached carefully; flee through the shelves if attacked — they know every hiding place in the corridor.

## Potential Treasure

> [!info] Plane of Water — Coralkin Gift (if bargained)
> *Tideglass Lens* — a coral-and-glass lens on a cord. Held to the eye, grants advantage on Survival checks to navigate any body of water and functions as *locate object*, 1/day, but only for objects submerged in water. Uncommon, no attunement.

> [!info] The Feywild — Baba Lysaga's Dropped Satchel
> Left behind when she shapechanged and fled (anything merely carried, not worn, doesn't transform with her):
> - Dark ritual components consistent with her blood-and-longevity rites.
> - A scrap of notation referencing ley-lines beneath the Ruins of Berez — a clue back to unfinished business there, not tradeable.
> - A small vial of preserved beast's blood.
> This was an ambush kit, not a treasure hoard — keep it thin and story-forward.

> [!info] Limbo — The Githzerai's Gift (if the trial succeeds)
> *Steadying Stone* — a smooth, faintly warm stone. While carried, advantage on Intelligence saving throws made to resist a plane's chaotic or reality-warping effects (Limbo's transformation checks, similar effects elsewhere). Uncommon, no attunement.

> [!info] The Great Library — Restricted Section
> *The Metaphysical Account* — a hand-copied transcription of Vecna's required Metaphysical Truth: the **Truth of Ending**. Tucked inside, a Servitor's note: someone accessed this section within the last month and left no footprints — no Servitor could recall their face when asked.
