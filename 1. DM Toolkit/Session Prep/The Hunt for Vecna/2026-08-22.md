## Strong Start — Two Doors Out of Bruokdon

> [!info] The Athenaeum Courtyard, Days After S44
> No recap needed beyond a line: the Bruokdon cell is broken, Marrow is dead, the dwarf who falsely confessed is still in a cell, and the amber gem in Marrow's corpse is still sitting uninvestigated wherever the party left it. Bruokdon itself has moved on faster than the party has — the official story making the rounds is "a dangerous impersonator," not a cult, and Velmont's council seat is safely his again.
>
> *Two messages find the party on the same morning.*
>
> The first is [[Professor Gousse Windcaller]], practically vibrating, waving the party into his office before they've finished crossing the courtyard — the Well of Many Worlds is ready, he's found his three resonant crossings, and he wants to leave for the Great Library *today*.
>
> The second is a folded note, delivered by a servant in Athenaeum livery who won't meet anyone's eyes: *Lord Velmont requests your presence at your earliest convenience. He has a proposal he believes you'll find worth your time.*

> [!quote] Gousse Windcaller
> "I've been thinking about the Well wrong. We both have. It doesn't open onto a plane at random, and it doesn't open onto whichever plane you'd *like*. It answers resonance. Open it somewhere the wind never stops, and it hears the Plane of Air. Open it in fire, it hears fire. You are not commanding the Well — you are *agreeing* with it about where you already are."
>
> "I've found three sites. A storm coast the old records blame for a century of missing ships — the Plane of Water answers there, I'd stake my chair on it. A fey ring no one's surveyed because no one who's gone looking has come back with a straight answer about what they found — that's the Feywild's ground, or close enough to it that the distinction won't matter. And a quarantined yard the Athenaeum has warned students off for longer than I've been alive, officially a magical hazard — I don't think that's all it is anymore. I think it's Limbo, bleeding through."
>
> "Three crossings, in that order — Water, then the Feywild, then Limbo. Then, and only then, the Library. Whenever you're ready — but I'd rather not wait long. Whoever's racing us there already has a head start."

**Route, for quick reference:** [[4. World Almanac/Worlds/Eldoria.md|Eldoria]] (Material Plane) → **Plane of Water** → **Feywild** → **Limbo** → **The Great Library**, crossed strictly in that order — Gousse's Well only opens one resonant crossing at a time, so there's no skipping ahead or backtracking mid-route. Gousse names all three planes outright in the quote above, so the party has full information to prep with (Water Breathing, cold-iron concerns in the Feywild, etc.) going in.

> [!info]- How the route actually works (have this ready if a player asks)
> The three sites aren't three separate errands from Bruokdon — they're one connected resonant thread, and their bad local reputations are literally the evidence Gousse used to find them, mined out of the borrowed survey records (Secret #3 below — he didn't discover the resonance principle, he found it already written down and went looking for places it applied). Each site's "reputation" is centuries of small bleed-through incidents on record:
> - **The storm coast** loses ships because the veil there is thin enough that the sea occasionally *is* the Plane of Water for a while. **This is the only physical journey the party makes** — a day or two's ride from Bruokdon (exact geography is a placeholder; Greenfall's map is still undeveloped).
> - **The fey ring** scrambles memory and truth in anyone who lingers, because it's Feywild bleed-through, not local ghost stories.
> - **The quarantined Athenaeum yard** has been off-limits "officially for hazard reasons" for generations because reality genuinely doesn't hold still there — it's Limbo, not old superstition.
>
> Once inside the Water plane, the coral-spire anchor isn't just a resting point — it's the water-side end of the *same* resonant thread that reaches the fey ring, so opening the Well there crosses straight to the Feywild with no need to backtrack to the coast or to Bruokdon. The same logic carries Feywild → Limbo (the standing stones and the githzerai enclave are two ends of one thread). The final leg, Limbo → Library, doesn't need a fourth physical site at all: the Library sits outside normal cosmology, and Limbo's raw, unformed matter is uniquely suited to being shaped into a door to somewhere that isn't really "a plane." **One overland trip to the coast, then an unbroken planar transit** — which is also why none of the four scenes below send the party back to Bruokdon in between.

> [!quote] Lord Caldris Velmont
> *He doesn't stand when you're shown in — a man conserving effort, not one being rude. There's a stillness to him that wasn't there before the chains.*
>
> "I imagine you're wondering why I'd want to see the people who dragged me out of my own basement in front of a stone golem and interrogated me for an afternoon. Fair. I'd wonder the same.
>
> "Here's the arithmetic. Whoever Marrow answered to still doesn't know he's dead. They've never seen his true face — only ever mine, whether I was the one wearing it or not. As far as anyone above Bruokdon is concerned, nothing has changed. Which means I can simply... resume. No disguise. No spell. Just walk back into my own life and see who reaches back.
>
> "One shipment's gone missing and hasn't been explained. That won't hold much longer. If you'd like to know where the rest of it was actually going, this is the only window you'll get, and it's closing."

**The choice is real, not a formality.** Gousse needs the party's full attention for a multi-day planar crossing; Velmont's window is a matter of days before the wider network stops waiting and either bolts or investigates on its own. Picking one doesn't burn the other — it defers it. Let the party ask questions of both, then commit. Jump to **Path A** or **Path B** below the instant they do.

If the party wants to putter in Bruokdon before committing to a path, these are ready to drop in — low stakes, no timer, no combat expected. Either can run standalone or both back to back.

> The Watch collected Marrow's body from the street in the days after S44; it's since been sitting in a custody room near the Athenaeum gate, tagged as evidence nobody's quite sure what to do with. Getting access is a formality, not an obstacle — the party are the heroes of the hour, and a word from Gousse or even Velmont opens the door (**DC 10 Persuasion** if anyone insists on rolling for it; otherwise just let it happen).

> - **DC 15 Arcana or Medicine:** confirms the graft is arcane, not natural, and structurally similar to accounts of the Amber Temple's craft — the same family of magic the party has already brushed against (the Stone Golem, S43).

He can't say why, only that it's *familiar*. This is the live thread into the Order of the Silver Dragon (Act II, Arc 3) surfacing early and organically, not resolving anything yet.
> - **If they extract or keep a fragment:** fine to allow — a small, inert shard of the graft, arcane but dormant. A physical memento and future hook, not a usable item.
> - Keep this short: a discovery-and-character scene, not a challenge. The payoff is the Redgrave beat and confirming the Amber Warden thread is real, not new information about Vecna.

> - No roll needed to notice it — Faerah's player should get this for free as a character beat.

# Path A — The Well of Many Worlds (The Great Library)

*Depends on: [[The Well of Many Worlds]], [[Professor Gousse Windcaller]], [[Baba Lysaga (COS)]], [[Mab]]. Reframed and enriched from the never-played 2026-07-18 draft.*

> [!info] Scene 1 — The Plane of Water

> [!quote] What They See
> The Well doesn't open so much as swallow. One heartbeat you're standing in Gousse's storm-lashed anchor site; the next, the sky itself has turned to water, and you're falling *up* into it — except there's no impact, no surface, just a seamless crossing from air into something vaster. Sunlight doesn't come from a sun here; it arrives in slow, twisting ropes of gold that seem to originate from everywhere at once, bending around towers of living coral the size of cathedrals, some breathing faint clouds of luminous spores that drift like slow-motion stars. Song moves through the water instead of air — a chorus of whalesong and something stranger underneath it, chords that seem to come from the current itself. Below the gold, the color gives out entirely into a blue-black so total it reads less like darkness and more like an absence carved into the world.
>
> Schools of fish shaped like fractured glass turn all at once, in perfect unison, tracing shapes that might be words in a language with no throat to speak it. Far below, past where your eyes give up entirely, something enormous changes the quality of the dark — not a shadow, but a *lack* moving through lack. It isn't coming toward you. Not yet.

> **Arrival:** The party surfaces mid-transition between the **Sea of Light** (sunlit, coral, inhabited) and the **Darkened Depths** (lightless, crushing). No fixed "up" — only "toward the gold" or "toward the black."
>
> **The clock is about pressure and disorientation, not air.** Every minute spent without reaching the resonant anchor (a coral spire/atoll), each character makes a **DC 12 Constitution save** (DC +1 each subsequent check) **or gains a level of exhaustion**. This is the plane's crushing depth and the vertigo of no fixed "up" working on the body and the nerves — it applies whether or not a character can breathe down here. A tension clock, not a trap — it punishes dawdling, not failed rolls. **Water Breathing does not stop this clock**, and neither does a swim speed; say so if anyone asks, since it's a reasonable assumption otherwise.
>
> **Reaching the anchor (splittable tasks):**
> - **Way-finding:** DC 13 Survival or Perception to read the current toward the Sea of Light. Failure just burns a minute, no harm.
> - **Swimming it:** DC 14 [[/5. Mechanics/Rules/Skills.md#Athletics|Strength (Athletics)]] against the current — automatic only for a creature with an actual **swim speed**. Help action works normally.
>
> **Expected prep — if the party casts Water Breathing before crossing:** they should. Gousse is guiding them deliberately and would tell them the first crossing is water-resonant if asked — this isn't a gotcha, reward the foresight. It removes drowning as a fail-state entirely (nobody suffocates on a failed clock check or a bad swim roll) and lets them stop and think without panicking. What it does *not* remove: the exhaustion clock (pressure/vertigo, not oxygen), the DC 14 swim check against the current for anyone without a swim speed, or the thing in the dark. Narrate the clock explicitly as crushing weight and a spinning inner ear rather than a gasp for air, so it still reads as dangerous to a party that can breathe fine. A character who racks up exhaustion from this scene is groggy and weak going into Scene 2, not drowned — the cost is real, just not lethal on its own.
>
> **Opportunity — Coralkin Scouts (friendly, optional):** Aquatic scouts loyal to the marids — skin like polished nautilus shell, hair that moves independent of any current — investigate the "sky-fallen" travelers. Curious, not hostile. They'll guide the party straight to the anchor — skipping the clock entirely — for something genuinely novel from the surface: a story, a bit of parlor magic, a plant they've never seen. Cheap to give, good roleplay, and the cleanest way to fully neutralize the scene if you'd rather not lean on the clock at all.
>
> **The thing in the dark:** Never statted, never meant to be fought. It only closes distance if the party racks up 3+ failed clock checks or throws loud, aggressive magic into the water. If it starts closing in, that's the cue to reach the anchor *now* — a chase beat, not a combat encounter. (If it ever needs real stats because the table forces the issue, reflavor [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Dragon/Dragon Turtle.md|Dragon Turtle]], CR 17 — a name that should stay firmly out of the players' hands as a threat to actually fight.)

> [!info] Scene 2 — The Feywild

> [!quote] What They See
> The Well opens onto a dusk that has forgotten how to become night. A sun that might not be a sun sits low and swollen and orange at the horizon, motionless no matter how long you watch it, throwing violet shadows through a wood too symmetrical to be wild and too alive to be planted — trees whose bark spirals in perfect fractal whorls, leaves that chime very faintly against each other like glass bells. Will-o'-the-wisps drift between the trunks at knee height, unhurried, occasionally pausing to circle a boot as if in greeting. Somewhere close, unseen, four notes repeat on an instrument none of you can name — pleasant, patient, never quite resolving, as though whoever plays it has all the time that exists and none of the urgency.
>
> The grass here is the wrong, too-vivid green of a dream you're about to wake from. At the center of a ring of standing stones — each one carved with a face too worn to read, too deliberate to be natural — a figure waits, unbothered by how long she's clearly been waiting. She was expecting you before you knew you were coming.

> **The trap, and why she bothers with it at all:** The figure is [[Baba Lysaga (COS)|Baba Lysaga]], wearing a fey glamour granted by [[Mab]] as part of their bargain. Lysaga is not a duelist and knows it — her whole approach to a fight is "smart, never fair." Before she ever springs an ambush on five people, she wants at least one of them already weakened, and a bargain sealed with "one true thing you've never said aloud" isn't a courtesy or a flourish — it's how the curse gets in. Play the offer completely straight and genuinely tempting; the cruelty is that it's a *real* offer, honestly delivered, with a cost that isn't mentioned because the fine print was never supposed to be read.
>
> **What's actually offered — pick one to dangle, worded warmly, like an old custom of the roads, not a threat:**
> - **Safe, guided passage** the rest of the way through the Feywild — skip this scene's fight and the standing-stone circle entirely.
> - **A name and a face** — real intel on who the Silent Ascendency sent ahead to the Great Library, and what they're after there.

> [!quote] The Broker's Offer — read this aloud as written
> "You've crossed a long way to stand in my ring, and further waits past it. I can make the rest of the road kinder to you than it means to be — kinder than you'd think to ask for.
>
> All I want in return is small. One true thing — the kind you've never let past your teeth. Say it here, just once, just to me, and I promise you'll set it down and not have to carry it any further.
>
> A truth spoken plainly weighs nothing once it's out in the open air. You'll feel lighter for it, I promise you that.
>
> That's all a fair road has ever asked of anyone. Say the word, and it's done — and so are you. Safely, and on your way."

> [!warning]- DM-only — decoding the offer (never explain this at the table; let it land later)
> Every line is true and every line is a trap, which is the whole point of fey wordplay — nothing here is a lie the party couldn't eventually catch on a re-read.
> - *"Kinder than you'd think to ask for"* — sounds like a bonus. Means: kinder in a way they wouldn't want if they understood it.
> - *"You'll set it down and not have to carry it any further"* — sounds like relief. Means: the truth isn't discarded, it's transferred — Lysaga now holds a leash woven from it. This is the disadvantage on Wisdom saves, mechanically: their own guard, given away.
> - *"Weighs nothing once it's out in the open air. You'll feel lighter"* — sounds comforting. Means: weightless is another word for unanchored, unguarded — exactly the state Baleful Baying wants them in.
> - *"That's all a fair road has ever asked"* — true and misleading at once: Feywild bargains genuinely are ancient and "fair" by fey law (Mab never breaks one). Fair was never the same promise as safe.
> - *"Say the word, and it's done — and so are you. Safely, and on your way."* — the sharpest one. "So are you" reads as "you're also done [with this, unharmed]" right up until the glamour drops, when it reads as "so are you — undone." Classic fey double-bind, and the line to callback if she gloats after the reveal.
>
> **Mechanic:** any character who actually completes the bargain (speaks a genuine truth aloud to seal it) takes **disadvantage on Wisdom saving throws for the remainder of the encounter** the instant they finish speaking — narrate it as that small, private weight settling into place, nothing dramatic yet. Multiple PCs can each strike their own bargain for a richer reward (more thorough intel, longer-lasting passage) — each one who speaks takes the curse individually; the party can knowingly trade more risk for more payoff.
>
> **Seeing through it early:** any character can attempt **Insight (DC 17, her spell save DC)** if something prompts suspicion (an odd word choice, a too-perfect answer) *before* anyone completes a bargain. Success reveals that the price isn't what it sounds like — enough to make an informed choice, not enough to name the exact mechanic. It doesn't stop the ambush on its own, but a character who saw through it isn't personally surprised when it springs, whether or not the party still decides to bargain anyway.
>
> **The reveal:** the instant a bargain is struck (or the party gets suspicious/aggressive enough to force it), the glamour drops — the too-perfect broker peeling away like wet paper to reveal Lysaga's true, hunched, hungry-eyed self, and the pleasant unmoving dusk suddenly feels like exactly what it is: a held breath. Everyone who didn't see through it is **surprised** for the first round. **Declining the bargain doesn't avoid the fight** — Lysaga sprung this trap the moment she had them in the ring; it only avoids the personal curse.
>
> **The fight — tilted on purpose:** terrain is unseelie bramble, difficult terrain for the party only, thorns that seem to lean into your steps. Round 1 opens with Baleful Baying scattering the party into it and away from each other; Lysaga follows with battlefield control (Evard's Black Tentacles/Cloudkill) from range on whoever gets isolated, never melee. At ~⅓ HP she Shapechanges into a swarm of insects and flees — anything merely carried, not worn, is dropped. **Driving her off is a full win; the kill isn't required.** The hounds don't flee with her — on loan from Mab, they fight until the encounter resolves, then vanish back to her service either way.

```encounter
name: Baba Lysaga's Trap
creatures:
  - 1: [[Baba Lysaga (COS)]]
  - 2: [[Yeth Hound (MPMM)]]
```
> **CR math (Lazy DM benchmark):** 5 PCs × level 10 = 50 total levels; deadly at CR sum > 25. Lysaga (11) + 2× Yeth Hound (4 each) = 19 — Hard, not Deadly, by raw CR; the ambush surprise, terrain, Baleful Baying scatter, and now the bargain-curse do the real work of tilting it. **Yeth Hound note:** damage immunity to nonmagical weapons unless silvered — check the party's current gear before running this. Baleful Baying is DC 13 Wis, 300-ft range, forces frightened targets to spend their whole move fleeing directly away — brutal against anyone with disadvantage on that save from the bargain. Track who bargained; it's a per-character tag for the rest of the fight, not a stat block change.

> **Mab arrives after:** *The bramble goes still all at once — thorns retracting, wisps freezing mid-drift — as something colder than the Feywild's dusk settles over the clearing.* She doesn't fight, doesn't threaten. A bargain was struck on her ground in her name without her present — that makes it hers to settle. Coolly gracious throughout; this is the party's introduction to her. Owls and ravens gather in the canopy above her, silent, and go still the moment she stops moving.

> [!quote] Mab
> "A bargain was struck on my ground, in my name, without my presence. That makes it mine to settle, whether or not she finishes what she started. You're not owed an apology. You're owed a choice. I'd like to make you one."

> **Mab's offer:** safe passage onward, no further fight, in exchange for:
> 1. **Immediate and personal — but not the same trick Lysaga played.** Mab doesn't need anyone to speak anything aloud, and doesn't trade in confessions pried loose with warm words — that's a broker's game, beneath her. She simply names what she's taking: a name that matters to the chosen PC but isn't their own — a parent's, a childhood nickname only one voice ever used, someone they've lost — and it's quietly gone from anyone else's power to use, for good or ill, until she chooses to give it back. No mechanical cost to the fight (it's already over by the time she arrives); this is a narrative marker with real weight — recoverable later through a quest, a *wish*, or another bargain with her court. **Deliberately worded differently from Lysaga's price** so the two don't blur together at the table: Lysaga tricks you into giving something up; Mab simply takes what she's owed, plainly, because she can and because it's hers to take by the terms of the bargain broken on her ground.
> 2. **A marker, unspecified** — one future favor, on her terms, at a time of her choosing. Don't define it now; it's a hook for later, once you know what would actually cost the party something to refuse.
>
> **If refused:** she doesn't force it — she simply doesn't smooth the way onward. The next crossing gets harder to find (DM's discretion on the complication). She's transactional, not cruel, and has nothing to gain from trapping them here.

> [!info] Scene 3 — Limbo

> [!quote] What They See
> The Well opens onto nothing holding still. A stretch of what was stone a heartbeat ago runs liquid silver, then rings like struck metal, then breathes out as smoke and comes down again as snow that was never cold enough to be snow. There is no ground to stand on and no sky to fall toward — you simply hang, suspended in a churn of half-finished reality, and moving is a matter of *deciding to*, the way you'd decide to lift a hand. Fragments of somewhere else tumble past at impossible, shifting distances: a stretch of hedge maze, a wedge of castle battlement mid-collapse, a stream of water running determinedly toward nothing, none of them connected to anything, none of them staying long enough to matter, all of them achingly, vertiginously real for the second they exist.
>
> Colors bleed into sounds here if you're not careful how you look at things. About four hundred feet distant, impossibly, something is *not* moving — a shape too regular for this place, square edges holding rigid against the churn like a held note in the middle of chaos, refusing entirely to participate in its own dissolution.

> **Arrival:** No gravity — movement is willed, not walked (a creature moves up to its speed in any direction just by intending it). The stable shape is a **githzerai enclave**, one of the "islands of invention" disciplined minds can hold together here.
>
> **The crossing — Power of the Mind (RAW Limbo mechanic):** Each round moving through unstabilized Limbo, a character not inside a stabilized bubble makes a **DC 13 Intelligence save** or the matter around them transforms mid-step — minor, non-lethal chaos (spun off course, briefly encased, a held item transforms and must be dropped or re-drawn). Alternatively, spend an action:
>
> > As an action, an Intelligence check to **stabilize a spherical area** centered on the creature: base DC 5 for a 10-ft. radius, +5 per additional 10 ft. Success holds the bubble for 24 hours or until reused.
>
> Play it as a leapfrogging puzzle — best INT throws the bubble, the party crosses into it, then pushes the next one forward. Rewards coordination over brute force.
>
> **Opportunity — the Githzerai Enclave (roleplay, avoidable):** [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Humanoid/Githzerai Monk.md|Githzerai Monks]] (CR 2) led by a [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Humanoid/Githzerai Zerth.md|Githzerai Zerth]] (CR 6) offer a stabilized shortcut to the next resonance point for a brief trial — not combat, composure: one representative PC must stabilize a 10-ft. bubble unassisted (DC 5, trivially easy — the trial is about doing it alone under scrutiny, not the number).
>
> **If the party is loud, careless, or slow:** chaos notices. A [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Aberration/Red Slaad.md|Red Slaad]] (CR 5) or [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Aberration/Blue Slaad.md|Blue Slaad]] (CR 7) — mottled, wet-skinned, wrong-jointed — drifts into view, trailing a wake of half-transformed stone in its passing. Dangerous solo, but under no obligation to engage anything not in its way. An avoid-or-flee beat, not a scripted fight. If the party impressed the enclave, the githzerai intervene to drive it off.

> [!info] Scene 4 — The Great Library

> [!quote] What They See
> The portal doesn't open onto a room so much as onto a held breath. Five walls meet in a foyer with dark stone underfoot, worn to a mirror-smooth hollow at its center by more footsteps than could ever be counted. Light falls from nowhere — not from any lamp or window, but from the air itself, which is thick with drifting motes that catch the glow like dust in a sunbeam, endlessly, though there is no sun and never was one. The smell is old paper, dry and clean, entirely without decay, as though time itself has agreed to move more carefully in here.
>
> Five corridors radiate out from the foyer like the points of a star, each lined floor to a thirty-foot vaulted ceiling with shelves that narrow toward a vanishing point that never actually arrives no matter how far you walk. At the exact center of the foyer, a fountain runs utterly without sound — its surface, when you look, doesn't show your own reflection but a shelf somewhere else in the Library, one none of you have found yet, waiting.

> **Corridors:** I. Magical Theory & Arcana, II. Historical & Religious Literature, III. The Multiverse, IV. Physics, Metaphysics & Everything In Between, V. Mathematics, Architecture & Miscellaneous.
>
> **Library mechanics:** Time is very slow — 1 day here = 7 days on the Material Plane. Evocation and Illusion spells have a 20% failure chance; Concentration on Illusion spells is DC 15 instead of 10.
>
> **Opportunity — The Nothics (Corridor II):** Two Nothics — hunched, single-eyed, murmuring constantly to themselves in a language made entirely of secrets — use Weird Insight on entry (WIS DC 13) to learn a secret from two party members. Not immediately hostile — they'll trade secrets and point toward a sympathetic Servitor rather than fight if approached carefully (DC 14 Persuasion for active cooperation).
>
> **What they'll trade, if approached carefully:** among the secrets they've gathered picking through Corridor II over the years, one is a genuine lead — a text on the founding of **the Eternal Watch** was quietly moved to the restricted section three years ago, and the Nothics have always wondered why. It's a real find, not a lie, offered because Nothics deal in secrets, not because they know what it means to the party. **A second, independent channel to the same clue:** even without engaging the Nothics, a **DC 15 History or Religion check** while browsing Corridor II's card catalog turns up the entry itself, cross-referenced and marked "moved" with no destination logged — the three-clue rule holds even if the party skips the Nothics entirely.
>
> **The payoff, once the restricted section is reached (see below) or the reference is followed up:** the Eternal Watch didn't just watch over Vecna's prison at the Amber Temple — historical record confirms **they built it and bound him there in the first place.** Wisp's master isn't only a guardian of the seal; he belongs to the order whose founders originally imprisoned Vecna. No roll needed to land the weight of this for Wisp's player — let it just be true, out loud, the moment the reference is read.
>
> **The founding text goes further, and this is the part that should reframe the master's coma:** an Eternal Watch seal isn't pure spellwork left running unattended — each generation's active Watcher binds a living thread of their own mind into it, both lock and lookout at once. As long as the Watcher's mind holds that thread, the seal holds, and the Watcher knows the instant it's tested. It's stronger than magic alone for exactly that reason — and it means that when the seal shattered, the backlash didn't just burn through a spell, it tore back up the thread and hit the Watcher's own mind directly. His body lives. His mind didn't just go dark — it folded in on itself around the shock of what he felt in that instant, and whatever he knew is very likely still in there, buried rather than gone. **This is why nothing mundane has fixed him** (Greater Restoration, Remove Curse, and the like all fail against this — it isn't a curse or a disease, it's a wound in the structure of his own mind) **and why this session should leave the party with a concrete new question: someone is going to have to go in after what's in there, not wait for him to wake up and hand it over.**
>
> **The hook forward, seeded but not resolved:** a marginal reference (not the main text — something a player has to go looking for, or the DM can simply volunteer once the core reveal lands) mentions that the Eternal Watch keeps, or once kept, a rite for exactly this failure mode — walking a living mind into a stricken Watcher's own, on purpose, to retrieve what's trapped there. No name, no mechanism, no cost given here; that belongs to Act II, Arc 4, when the party actually finds the Eternal Watch. **DM-only working name:** call it **the Hollow Vigil** if a placeholder is needed at the table tonight — not to be explained yet, just enough that "we need to find a way into his mind" becomes the party's own conclusion by the end of the scene, not something Gousse or a book tells them to do outright.
>
> **DM-only, tying it to the endgame:** whatever's buried in the master's mind is a strong candidate for the "specific knowledge from the Eternal Watch" the campaign bible's Endings section already gates behind Act II — both the worthier-claimant option (if he's the Watch's contingency candidate) and the domain-collapse option could plausibly live here. Don't commit to which, or to both, until Act II Arc 4 actually plays out.
>
> **DM-only:** why the founding text was restricted three years ago — years before Vecna's actual escape (S33) — is a deliberately open question. Plausible threads for later: the Silent Ascendency quietly working to bury the Eternal Watch's history in advance, or unrelated Athenaeum politics that only look sinister in hindsight. Don't resolve it tonight.
>
> **Opportunity — The Invisible Servitor:** Guards the restricted section below the fountain — felt more than seen, a cool displacement of air and the faint sound of a page turning where no page is visible. Grants access for a demonstrated scholarly purpose (not "we need to stop Vecna" alone — they must articulate *why* this specifically enables that), academic credibility (Arcana DC 18, advantage if they name Windcaller), or something the Library doesn't already contain.
>
> **The warning:** someone accessed the restricted section within the last month — "a robed humanoid who left no footprints, whose face no Servitor could recall when asked to describe it." The Silent Ascendency got here first.
>
> **The payoff:** *The Metaphysical Account* — the party's first real understanding of Vecna's actual goal: not immortality, but to become the **Truth of Ending**, the last thought in a dying universe.

# Path B — The Second Ledger (The Cinderworks Infiltration)

*Depends on: [[Lord Caldris Velmont]], [[Hesper Crane]], [[The Cinderworks]], [[Duskferry]], [[The Second Ledger]]. See [[Lord Caldris Velmont]]'s Secrets for why the ruse works and where Velmont's own agenda sits underneath it. [[Anselm Corrow]], [[Ostwin Reyes]], and [[Amberfen Hollow]] are built for a future Duskferry session, not tonight — don't surface those names or that location yet.*

> [!info] Scene 1 — The Pitch

Velmont lays out the plan cold and matter-of-fact (see Strong Start quote above for his opening). Let the party push back, negotiate, and set terms — he expects it and won't be offended by hard questions.

- **What he'll agree to:** the party posing as his new escort ("the old muscle got picked up in the sweep" is close enough to true), a signal or check-in method so the party can bail him out if it goes wrong, and no promises about what happens to the operation *after* — he's cagey on that by design.
- **What he won't do:** wear a disguise, use magic to alter his appearance (there's nothing to hide — that's the whole point), or agree to hand over control of the plan once it's moving. He's leading; the party is backup.
- **If the party presses on his motive:** he doesn't pretend to be reformed. "I want the people who put a leash on me and wore my face for a year to answer for it. Whatever you get out of this is yours. I'm not doing this for you." True, and all he'll admit to.
- **If the party refuses outright or threatens him into folding:** he doesn't beg — he simply says the window's closing and ends the meeting. This path stays open a short while (he hasn't gone anywhere yet) but the DM can let it lapse into Path A if the party doesn't come back to it.

> [!info] Scene 2 — The Approach

> [!quote] What They See
> The Cinderworks doesn't look abandoned so much as it looks like it's holding still on purpose. Twelve years of Bruokdon weather have stripped the paint from its condemned signage but left the old foundry's iron bones intact — three cracked smokestacks against the evening sky, windows boarded from the inside rather than out, and a smell on the wind that shouldn't still be there this long after a fire: cold ash, and underneath it, something like ozone. The disused stretch of the outer wall it backs onto is exactly as unwatched as Velmont promised, and exactly as unwatched as it shouldn't be for a city this careful about its wards.

- A single watcher covers the old delivery tunnel mouth. **DC 14 Stealth** (or Velmont openly walking up and being recognized — his call, his risk) gets the party into position; failure just costs time and a tense conversation Velmont has to talk his way through, not an immediate fight.
- Inside the tunnel: scorched warding sigils on the stone, old but re-touched — a detail worth an **Arcana DC 15** if anyone thinks to check: this place is still being actively maintained against divination, not just decaying on its own.

> [!info] Scene 3 — The Quartermaster

[[Hesper Crane]] meets "Velmont" in the old foundry floor, ledger under one arm, immediately mentioning the missing shipment. Run this as a social encounter with real stakes, not a binary pass/fail.

**Objective:** get the ledger (or its contents) without a fight — or get it anyway if the fight happens.

**Obstacle 1 — the password.** Marrow set up a standing verification phrase with Crane that Velmont was never told. She asks for "the word" almost as a formality — until she doesn't get it.
- **DC 16 Deception** (Velmont leads; a party member assisting with a good angle — insight into her paranoia, a plausible excuse — grants advantage): **Success** — he waves it off with irritation ("after the week I've had, you're testing me?") and she lets it go, suspicious but not yet acting on it. **Failure** — she doesn't call it out immediately, but quietly repositions near her guards. Move to Obstacle 2 with disadvantage on it.

**Obstacle 2 — the missing shipment.** She wants an explanation for why it never arrived.
- **DC 15 Deception or Persuasion:** **Success** — the cover story (a guard crackdown got the local muscle, "Velmont" barely got clear) holds; she opens the ledger and talks business. **Partial success (fails Obstacle 1, succeeds here):** she stays wary but cooperative — she'll deal, but watches the party closely and won't turn her back. **Failure on both:** she demands proof of identity she knows "Velmont" can't produce and moves to detain him — straight into Scene 4's fight.

**A third option, always available:** Crane answers to whoever pays on time, not to any cause. A bribe or a cut of future business (**DC 13 Persuasion backed by a real offer**) buys her cooperation regardless of how the first two checks went — fail-forward, not a dead end.

> [!info] Scene 4 — If It Breaks

If both social obstacles fail and no bribe is offered, Crane moves to detain "Velmont" and the fight starts. This should feel like a real fight, not a formality — but it's this session's speed bump, not its climax.

> **CR math (Lazy DM benchmark):** 5 PCs × level 10 = 50 total levels; deadly at CR sum > 25. Blackguard (8) + 2× Veteran (3 each = 6) + 2× Thug (½ each = 1) = 15 — a real fight with decent action-economy parity (5 combatants vs. 5 PCs), but comfortably under the Path A Baba Lysaga fight (19) and well short of deadly, appropriate for a secondary op rather than tonight's potential climax.

> **Tactics:** Crane leads from the back with Aura of Hate boosting her guards, and disengages the moment the fight turns clearly against her rather than dying for an employer who's stopped answering her messages (see her Secrets). The Veterans hold the tunnel chokepoint; the Thugs are mundane muscle, not fanatics, and will flee once one of them drops.
> **Morale:** Crane surrenders rather than fights to the death if isolated or reduced below half HP — a live source for the Duskferry lead even if the social scene failed outright.

> [!info] Scene 5 — The Find

Whether won by words or by force, the payoff is the same: Crane's ledger and the cache itself.

- **The ledger** names a Ferrymen's Guild contact in [[Duskferry]] by code name only — **"the Tallyman"** — the concrete next step in the network above Bruokdon. The real name ([[Ostwin Reyes]]) and the honest Guildmaster who has no idea his own clerk is compromised ([[Anselm Corrow]]) are DM-only until a future session actually follows the lead — don't volunteer either name tonight.
- **The cache** — see Potential Treasure below.
- **Optional deeper find:** a locked crate in the loft office holds an inert, half-formed soul-vessel core — Amber Warden material, the same craft as the graft still sitting in [[Marrow]]'s uninvestigated corpse. Not required to resolve tonight; a live thread either way.
- **Velmont's exit:** he's satisfied, sharper and looser than the party has seen him — this is the win he wanted. Play one small beat of him pocketing something from the ledger or cache that he doesn't mention, if the DM wants to plant the seed of his own agenda without forcing it further tonight (see his Secrets).
