## Strong Start — Velmont's Send-Off

> [!info] The Umbral Athenaeum, the Morning After
> No recap needed beyond a line: [[The Cinderworks]] is cleared, [[Hesper Crane]] is in custody and has given up everything she knows, and [[Lord Caldris Velmont|Velmont]] has resumed his council seat without a ripple. The only loose thread with a name on it is a code name — **"the Tallyman"** — and a phrase Crane swore would get a response from him: *"The day has been long, but the evening even longer."*
>
> Velmont doesn't go with them this time. He's blunt about why.

> [!quote] Lord Caldris Velmont
> "I resumed a council seat two days ago. I am not explaining to the Institute why Lord Velmont is standing in a Duskferry tally-house asking a clerk for a password. You are, as far as anyone downriver needs to know, Crane's people — the ones who survive when things go wrong and still show up to finish the job. That's worth more to a man like the Tallyman than my face ever would be.
>
> Take her ledger. Take the phrase. Tell him Cinderworks had 'trouble' — that word, no more — and that the schedule needs sorting out in person because Crane's gone quiet. If he's as careful as she made him sound, he'll want to see you as much as you want to see him."

**The pretext, for quick reference:** the party travels as Crane's replacements — battle-worn, discreet, carrying her actual ledger as credentials — to explain a "disruption" at the Cinderworks and re-establish contact with whoever answers to the code phrase. No physical shipment is needed; Reyes's whole operation runs on paperwork and trust, not crates changing hands in daylight. This isn't a formality — Reyes doesn't know Crane is captured, and a wrong move here costs the lead entirely, not just tonight's convenience.

## Scene 1 — The Row to Duskferry

*Depends on: [[The Cinderworks]], [[Duskferry]]. A full day's hard pull downriver — no random-encounter padding needed; the toll here is fatigue, not danger.*

> [!quote] What They See
> Nobody signs anything. The skiff waiting behind the Cinderworks' delivery tunnel is the same low, unmarked thing Crane's own couriers use to move cargo without ever touching the Guild's toll ledger — which is exactly the point. No ticket, no manifest, no record that anyone left Bruokdon at all. The spires fall behind by midmorning; what's left after that is river, reed-bank, and the long pull downstream.

**Why they're rowing it themselves:** buying passage on an actual Ferrymen's Guild ferry would put five armed strangers on a logged manifest before they've even reached Duskferry — the opposite of the quiet, off-the-books arrival Velmont's plan depends on. The skiff is slower and harder, but it's how "Crane's people" would actually travel.

**The toll:** it's most of a day's hard rowing. Whoever takes a real shift at the oars makes a **DC 13 Constitution save** at the end of the day or gains one level of [[5. Mechanics/Rules/Conditions.md#Exhaustion|exhaustion]] from the exertion — a plain physical grind, not a hazard with teeth. Rotating shifts (three or more people spelling each other) means nobody has to row the whole way alone; anyone who sits the trip out entirely — steering, keeping watch, resting — doesn't roll at all. If the party wants to push and shave a couple of hours off the arrival, that's available narratively, but anyone still on the oars rolls with disadvantage for the extra effort.

Arrival lines up with Scene 2 below either way — they reach Duskferry as the fog is already rolling in for the evening.

## Scene 2 — Arrival at Duskferry

*Depends on: [[Duskferry]].*

> [!quote] What They See
> Bruokdon announces itself with spires; Duskferry announces itself with smell — tar, river mud, and woodsmoke, rolling upriver on air thick enough to chew. The town isn't built so much as stacked: three tiers on pilings above a flood line that clearly wins some years. Ferry slips groan at the **Waterline**, loudest and lowest, where dockhands sing cargo counts over each other and nobody looks up twice at a new face. Above it, the **Duckboards** — a raised timber walk of taverns, tally-houses, and rooms let by the week — is where the town actually lives, packed close enough that every door is somebody's business. Higher still, and the only structure here built on ground that doesn't flex underfoot, sits the **Guildhall Roost**: squat, deliberate stonework, the one clear statement in this whole town about whose foundation is sturdiest.
>
> Fog comes in off the river most evenings thick enough to swallow lantern light within twenty feet. Tonight it's already rolling in early, and the Guild, as ever, hasn't said a word against it.

**The choice, stated plainly:** the tally-house that actually handles manifests is inside the Guildhall Roost — reachable two ways. Walk in the front, through Guildmaster [[Anselm Corrow]], and ask for help finding a name that isn't on any public ledger. Or work the Duckboards and Waterline first, asking around and reading whatever's left in plain sight, before ever setting foot in the Roost. Neither burns the other — Corrow doesn't know he has a problem yet either way.

## Scene 3 — Anselm Corrow (if approached directly)

*Depends on: [[Anselm Corrow]]. He is not Ascendency and does not know what "special cargo" is — but he is not a soft touch either. A group of strangers walking into his office with an accusation against his own Guild and a ledger he's never seen the other half of should earn real skepticism, not instant trust.*

Corrow receives visitors in a cramped office that smells like tar and old paper, a private ledger of favors open on the desk beside the Guild's official one. He doesn't reach for the ledger they offer, or even look at it closely at first — he looks at *them*.

> [!quote] Anselm Corrow
> "Every season somebody walks through that door with a grudge and a story. Half of them are lying to me. The other half believe every word and are still wrong. You want me to open my own archives on the strength of a book I've never seen the other half of, from people I've never met, about a clerk who's worked this hall longer than some of you have been alive. Convince me."

**What he actually wants before anything is granted:** where the ledger came from, why Crane herself isn't the one standing here, and proof the party knows what they're talking about rather than just what they're claiming.

- **DC 16 Persuasion** (his default skepticism sets the DC higher than a friendly ask would) **or DC 13 Investigation demonstrated on the spot** (point out a specific, real irregularity in his own public manifests without being shown where to look first — proof of knowledge beats proof of good intentions with him): **Success** — he's convinced enough to act, but on his terms, not theirs (see below). **Failure** — he doesn't believe them outright, but see the Partial option; he isn't finished with them yet.
- **If the party is honest that they got the ledger by force** (interrogating Crane in Bruokdon): he doesn't recoil, but it costs something — a Lawful Neutral Guildmaster hearing that outsiders extracted evidence from someone tied to his docks through violence is a genuine complication for him, not a footnote. Fold it into the roll above as disadvantage unless they can also frame it as legitimate (city Watch involvement, a real crime uncovered) rather than pure vigilantism.
- **Full success:** Corrow doesn't fling the archives open and walk away — he insists on going through the manifests **himself, with the party present**, not handing them free rein. This is a complication as much as a win: if the trail points at a specific clerk mid-search, he wants to march up and confront them immediately, and the party will need to talk him into patience (a quick DC 13 Persuasion, or just good roleplay) if they want to handle Reyes carefully rather than have Corrow blow the approach.
- **Partial (fails the roll but doesn't lie or threaten him):** he won't open the archives today. He's rattled enough to look into it himself, quietly, on his own timeline — which means Clue 2 isn't available through him this session, but his own quiet inquiries start ticking (DM's call whether this reaches Reyes before, during, or after Scene 5 — a soft clock, not an immediate alarm).
- **If the party is cagey, threatening, or caught in a lie:** Corrow shuts the conversation down — not hostile, just done. He won't help smugglers, real or suspected, and a Guildmaster who feels lied to is exactly the kind of person his own note says he can't stand being. This doesn't lock the party out of Duskferry, just out of his goodwill and his archives (Clue 2 gets harder — DC 15 becomes DC 18 without official access, or requires breaking in after hours) — and he may mention the odd visit to his senior staff afterward "to be careful," a quiet way the clock on Reyes noticing something is off can start ticking even on a failure.
- **If the party asks about "the Tallyman" by name first:** he's never heard the term. It isn't a Guild title or a nickname he recognizes — a true, useful dead end that rules out the obvious guess (that it's him) without wasting much time.

## Scene 4 — Finding the Tallyman

*The three-clue rule in action — any one is enough to point at [[Ostwin Reyes]]; two or more let the party approach him already knowing, rather than fishing blind.*

> [!tip] The Three Clues
> 1. **Crane's ledger** (already in hand): the code name "the Tallyman" appears in a pattern matching her own password arrangement — a rhythm the party can recognize now that they've seen it once.
> 2. **The tally-house's own records** (**DC 15 Investigation**, reviewed alongside a won-over, watching Corrow, or taken alone by stealth/bribery): a small, consistent set of manifests have sequential entries missing — gaps that all fall in the same clerk's hand.
> 3. **Ostwin Reyes himself**, confronted or pressured carefully (see Scene 5) — he can be made to confirm it, though it takes real leverage, not just a direct question. His pride in never being caught is exactly what makes him overconfident that he won't be.

**If the party skips investigation entirely:** they can simply ask around the tally-house for whichever clerk handles "irregular" freight and speak the phrase to whoever seems most plausible. Wrong guesses cost time and a confused, innocent clerk's afternoon — not danger. Reyes himself, once actually addressed with the phrase, has no way to bluff past it (see Scene 5) — the phrase alone is enough to identify him, once they're standing in front of him.

## Scene 5 — Ostwin Reyes

*Depends on: [[Ostwin Reyes]]. Run this as a social encounter with real stakes, same shape as Crane's interrogation in S45 — not a binary pass/fail.*

> [!quote] What They See
> Narrow, ink-stained hands. A guild coat cut a rank above his station. A man who corrects your arithmetic before he's finished agreeing with you — and who goes very, very still the instant the phrase leaves someone's mouth.

**Objective:** confirm he's the contact, get the next link in the chain (the route toward [[Greyfen Hollow]]) — without a fight if possible, or through pressure if not.

**Obstacle 1 — proving they're really Crane's people.** Reyes has never met Crane in person either — everything's gone through couriers — so he can't catch a face he's never seen. What he can catch is unfamiliarity with the *arrangement's texture*: exact phrasing, timing, the ledger's actual handwriting.
- **DC 15 Deception (with the ledger as a prop, advantage if they lead with the phrase unprompted):** **Success** — he's satisfied enough to talk business, still watching for a slip. **Failure** — he doesn't call it out outright (too careful for that), but quietly moves toward the door to the clerks' floor "for air." Move to Obstacle 2 with disadvantage.

**Obstacle 2 — explaining "trouble" at the Cinderworks.** He wants to know exactly what kind of trouble, and why Crane herself isn't the one telling him.
- **DC 15 Deception or Persuasion:** **Success** — a version close to the truth (a guard sweep, Crane laying low, the party sent to keep the schedule from slipping) satisfies him; he confirms he's "the Tallyman" and talks route logistics. **Partial (fail Obstacle 1, succeed here):** he stays cooperative but cold, and quietly signals a courier to "check on things" upriver the moment they leave — a complication for a future session, not tonight's fight. **Failure on both:** he decides they're either impostors or a liability either way, and moves to have them removed — straight into Scene 5.

**A third option, always live:** Reyes's own flaw and bond both point the same way — money talks louder than loyalty. **A direct, well-pitched bribe or cut (DC 13 Persuasion backed by a real offer)** gets his cooperation regardless of how the checks above went. He's proud, not principled.

**What he actually knows, once talking:** the route onward from Duskferry runs downriver into the marsh, to a site he's never seen and never asked about — he only knows it as "the Hollow." He genuinely doesn't know what happens there, and says so without needing to be pressed hard on that specific point — it reads as true because it is (see his Secrets).

## Scene 6 — If It Breaks

If both social obstacles fail with no bribe offered, or the party forces the issue too hard, Reyes has his people move — not to fight so much as to buy him time to vanish through the tally-house's back channels.

```encounter
name: The Tally-House Breaks
creatures:
  - 1: [[Ostwin Reyes]]
  - 2: [[Veteran]]
  - 4: [[Guard]]
```
> **CR math (Lazy DM benchmark):** 5 PCs × level 10 = 50 total levels; deadly at CR sum > 25. Reyes (1) + 2× Veteran (3 each = 6) + 4× Guard (⅛ each = 0.5) = **7.5** — a real scuffle, but deliberately lighter than S45's Cinderworks breakout (15) and well under tonight's Greyfen Hollow fight (10). This is a speed bump and a moral wrinkle, not a climax.
> **Terrain:** the tally-house's fogged-in Duckboards walkway — stacked ledger crates as cover, lantern light cutting maybe twenty feet through the fog, and a walkway plank or two that can be kicked loose to drop a pursuer toward the water below.
> **Tactics:** Reyes never fights — he peels off toward a side dock the instant the noise starts, using his Cunning Action-equivalent mobility to disengage and vanish into the fog. The two Veterans are his personal, paid enforcers and fight to cover his exit, not to win. The four Guards are Corrow's honest watch, drawn by the noise and genuinely believing they're stopping an attack on Guild property — not Ascendency-aligned, not fanatics, and a strong candidate for a non-lethal or talked-down resolution if the party can make the accusation against Reyes stick out loud, mid-fight.
> **Morale:** Guards break off and demand explanations the moment Reyes's guilt is shown convincingly (a captured Veteran's testimony, the ledger itself, anything concrete) rather than fighting to the last. The Veterans flee once Reyes is clear or they're bloodied — they're paid, not devoted.
> **Fail-forward:** even if Reyes escapes clean, he drops (or a Veteran carries and loses) a **waxed courier's chit** marked only with a stylized amber sigil and a rough downriver bearing — enough to find [[Greyfen Hollow]] without him. The lead survives a blown confrontation; it just gets messier.

## Side Beat — Faerah's Pendant & the Dusk Elves

*Depends on: [[Faerah Duskrane]], [[Duskferry]], [[Drow Eilistraee (MTF)|Eilistraee]]. A self-contained character beat for the Duskferry overnight. It **seeds** the hidden Eilistraee congregation quest flagged in the campaign bible (Character Tie-Ins → Faerah; Active Threads → "Eilistraee shrine"; candidate slot Act I, Arc 2) without launching it. Fully optional and compressible — run it as a 10–15 minute downtime scene while the party waits out the fog, or lift it to a later Duskferry visit if tonight runs long. Don't let it compete with the Reyes scene or push Greyfen Hollow off the table.*

### The trigger
Duskferry's evening fog breaks for a short while — the first true, unobstructed moonlight to fall on Faerah's pendant since Barovia. The crescent-in-a-circle stone takes the light and **holds** it, glowing a cold moonlight-blue, and pulls — faintly but unmistakably — toward a point out past the last of the pilings, downshore of the Waterline. Clear moon is rare here (the fog is near-nightly), so the moment reads as pointed, not incidental.

### The dusk elves
A small community of dusk elves lives at the edge of Duskferry — twilight-skinned, soft-spoken, not shunned by the townsfolk but keeping mostly to their own. *(Aesthetic only — no Ravenloft or mechanical "dusk elf" baggage; they're simply a reclusive elf enclave, and the name rhymes with the town and with Duskrane by design.)* They tend a small shrine to Eilistraee — **a low, weathered ring of standing stones on a reed-bank spit a short walk downshore from the Waterline**, half-screened by alders and easy to pass without a second look. Coming in over the water, or working the Duckboards, Faerah hears a few of them singing a worn old verse passed down among them:

> *What the shadow takes, it only keeps*
> *until the turning of the light;*
> *the moon you buried does not sleep —*
> *it walks the far side of the night.*

Flag it for the player. It sounds like a mourning-hymn. It pays off at the prayer.

> [!note] Settled
> - **The order:** the **Unbroken Circle** — the ring around the pendant's crescent is the promise that the line of bearers is never broken. The shrine is literally a ring of stones; the symbol and the sanctuary are the same shape.
> - **The shrine:** a low, weathered stone circle on a reed-bank spit a short walk downshore of the Waterline — old, modest, tended by the dusk elves, easy to miss.
> - **The elder:** **Naerissa**.
> - **Still open:** Faerah's sister has no name yet campaign-wide. Not needed tonight (goddess and elder both refer to her obliquely); owed before Act II, Arc 2.

### The revelation (concrete — this is the "backstory beat")
One of the dusk elves — the elder, **Naerissa** — was brought out of the Underdark years ago by **Faerah's sister**, part of a small group she guided to a surface exit. The sister did not leave with them. Naerissa recognises Faerah's pendant on sight: her sister wore its twin.

What Faerah gets from this:
- Her sister's escape was **not** the private dream the two of them shared in secret. She was already doing the work — for strangers — and was bound to something older than herself.
- The pendant is a **bearer-mark** of the **Unbroken Circle**: one of a set, given to those entrusted to lead others out of tyranny. Its power grows with the wearer. (Naerissa wears none herself — she was led out, not marked to lead.)
- Freeing Barovia is what **woke** Faerah's stone. Naerissa frames it, gently, as an initiation she passed without knowing she was being tested.

### The prayer at the shrine
If Faerah kneels and prays, Eilistraee answers directly — full monologue below. Two payloads:
1. **A charge to lead from the front** — stop carrying the frightened on her back where they can't see her; walk ahead where they can. (Faerah's arc: survivor → someone who wields hope and courage, not someone who quietly endures.)
2. **The sister-clue**, delivered by the goddess singing the dusk elves' verse back to her, note for note. The repetition is the tell. It reads as grief-comfort now; the literal meaning — **her sister is alive** — is reserved for Act II, Arc 2 and must **not** be confirmed here. If pressed outright, Eilistraee only ever sings the verse again.

> [!quote] Eilistraee, at the shrine
> *(The fog off the river thins for a few breaths and the moon stands clear. The stone at Faerah's throat takes the light and holds it — a small, cold coal of blue. The voice is not above her or inside her. It is beside her, low, the way you sing to someone who is almost asleep.)*
>
> "Faerah. You have worn my sign against your heart all these years and called it a keepsake. It was never a keepsake. It is a key, and a promise, and it has been waiting for you to be worth it.
>
> You were told once that you were dangerous to the web. You were. You still are. Every soul you have cut loose from a cruel hand — in the dark below, in Barovia's grey country, on this river tonight — was a thread pulled out of it. That land is free because you would not leave anyone behind in it. That was the proving. The stone heard it. The stone woke.
>
> Here is what waking asks of you. You have carried frightened people through every dark I have watched you cross — on your back, where they could not see your face. That was mercy. It was also hiding. Walk in front of them now. Let them see the fear on your face and let them follow it anyway. A turned back says *be still*. A face says *come with me*. You were not made to be a wall between people and the dark. You were made to be the one who says *this way*, and means it.
>
> And the one who clasped this chain at your throat, and told you to look up —
>
> *(and here the goddess sings, and it is the dusk elves' verse, note for note:)*
>
> *What the shadow takes, it only keeps*
> *until the turning of the light;*
> *the moon you buried does not sleep —*
> *it walks the far side of the night.*
>
> Look up, Faerah. You have been grieving toward the ground."

### The pendant, awakened — no mechanics
Purely narrative, by DM's call. The player floated a "seek guidance once per long rest" ability; it is deliberately **not** being granted as a codified resource — the pendant's power stays a story beat the DM leans on when it serves, not a button.
- The stone now **catches and holds moonlight**, glowing a soft cold blue whenever moon- or starlight touches it (and only then). Before tonight it never did this.
- That glow is how it **led her to the shrine**, and it's the visible tell on the connection she's had since S14 — *when the moon is out, Faerah hears Eilistraee*.
- No light radius, no action, no uses. If it ever needs to matter mechanically, that's a future conversation.

### Forward purpose (the "going forward")
Faerah leaves Duskferry with an awakened bearer-pendant and a named charge from her goddess. The dusk elves point her onward: there are *more of us, and older* — a larger hidden congregation of the **Unbroken Circle** out in the Greenfall wilds — but they "don't go there," and won't say much more. That is the seed of the campaign bible's Act I, Arc 2 Eilistraee-congregation quest (with its corrupted-convert infiltrator) — **not** a trip to take tonight. Open a quest note for the thread after this session — *[working title TBD]*.

## Scene 7 — The River Road to Greyfen Hollow

*Depends on: [[Greyfen Hollow]]. A half-day's pole downriver from Duskferry — no random-encounter padding needed; the toll here is fatigue, not danger.*

> [!quote] What They See
> The river gives out by degrees. Docks give way to reeds, reeds give way to standing dead trees, and the current itself slows to something closer to held breath than flow. Nobody local will name this stretch without spitting first. The Guild's own charts mark it with a blank space and one old convention: *don't.*

If the party asks around Duskferry before leaving, nobody will guide them — not out of malice, just old, well-earned fear. A **DC 12 Survival or Nature check** shaves an hour off the approach by reading the current correctly; failure just costs time, not danger.

**The same toll as the row down from Bruokdon (Scene 1), same shape:** the marsh doesn't reward a paddle the way open river does — reeds, deadfall, and half-solid mud drag at every stroke. Anyone taking a real shift at the oars/pole makes a **DC 12 Constitution save** at the end of the approach or gains one level of [[5. Mechanics/Rules/Conditions.md#Exhaustion|exhaustion]] — a shorter haul than the first leg, but thicker going, so the DC holds rather than dropping. Same mitigations: rotate shifts, and whoever isn't rowing doesn't roll.

## Scene 8 — Greyfen Hollow: The Drowned Approach

> [!quote] What They See
> Grey trunks rise out of black water that never quite freezes and never quite warms, close enough together that the boat has to thread rather than cut through. Nothing living calls out here — no birds, no insects, nothing but the occasional wet settling sound of something shifting underwater that never quite surfaces. At the center of the maze, on a rise of dry ground that has no business existing in a marsh this flat, sits a squat structure of fitted stone — old, deliberate, and far too well-made to have been raised by whoever's using it now.

**Fantastic location features (2–4 live details, use what fits the pace):**
- **The tree-maze itself** is difficult terrain for anyone without a swim speed or careful poling; a **DC 12 Survival** check finds the cleanest line through, failure costs time and noise.
- **Sinkhole ground** at the structure's edge — deceptively solid-looking silt that isn't. Anyone moving off the obvious approach without checking risks a **DC 13 Dexterity save** or sinking to the waist, restrained until freed (DC 12 Strength, or help from an ally).
- **The stonework itself** predates the Ascendency by a wide margin — a detail worth an **Arcana or History DC 15** if anyone stops to look, confirming what everyone's instinct already says: nobody currently using this place built it.
- **The oppressive quiet** is a detail worth playing for its own sake, not a mechanic — this is a place instinct says to be quiet in, and the DM should let the players lead with quiet approaches if they choose to.

## Scene 9 — The Amber Warden

*Depends on: [[Amber Warden]]. The first Amber Warden the party meets in person, rather than hears about secondhand through Marrow's neck-graft.*

> [!quote] What They See
> It doesn't wake so much as notice. A shape of fitted grey stone, roughly a man's outline scaled up half again, seams running through it like a golem's — except for the three points of amber light at its shoulders and chest, pulsing slow and wrong, like something breathing on a rhythm that isn't quite a heartbeat. When it moves, you can hear it: not stone grinding on stone, but a thin chorus of overlapping voices from somewhere inside, wordless, and very tired.

```encounter
name: The Amber Warden
creatures:
  - 1: [[Amber Warden]]
```
> **CR math (Lazy DM benchmark):** 5 PCs × level 10 = 50 total levels; deadly at CR sum > 25. A solo CR 10 sits at **10** — Medium by the raw sum, well under deadly. **Action economy flag:** one monster's turn against five PCs' turns is normally a mismatch waiting to happen — mitigated here by three things already built into the fight, not by adding legendary actions: (1) the marsh terrain above (difficult ground, the sinkhole hazard) slows positioning before the fight even starts; (2) [[Amber Warden|its Amber Conduits]] give the party a secondary objective that rewards splitting attention rather than just alpha-striking the main HP pool; (3) this is deliberately built as a hard gatekeeper fight, not tonight's climax — if the table wants more teeth, the cleanest add is one or two reskinned [[5. Mechanics/Bestiary/Humanoid/Guard.md|Guard]]s or a marsh hazard (rising water, unstable footing mid-fight) rather than legendary actions on the Warden itself.
> **Terrain:** the dry stone rise is the only solid, unobstructed ground — expect the fight to gravitate there. The tree-maze at its edges offers partial cover and difficult terrain for anyone pushed back into the water.
> **Tactics:** the Warden doesn't pursue past the rise's edge — it's a guardian, not a hunter, and won't chase a retreating party into the maze. It opens with Multiattack on whoever's closest, uses Bound Wail the instant it recharges regardless of positioning (it isn't tactically clever, just relentless), and fights until destroyed or until all three Amber Conduits are gone and someone forces the issue.
> **The tell:** a **DC 13 Arcana or Investigation** check (or simply asking "can we hit those amber lights?") identifies the conduits as targetable weak points before or during the fight — this is meant to be findable, not a hidden gotcha.

## Scene 10 — The Find

Whether the Warden falls or the party talks/sneaks past it (it can't be reasoned with, but it also won't chase — a full retreat is a legitimate way to end this scene), the stone structure's interior is the same discovery.

> [!quote] What They See
> Inside, the old stonework gives way to something newer and worse: a workroom bolted and grafted into the ancient bones of the place, alchemical apparatus and fine tools laid out with the same fussy precision as a tally-house ledger. Along one wall, a rack of inert amber cores sit half-formed — smaller cousins of the ones in the Warden outside, dark and quiet, waiting for something that hasn't arrived yet.

- **Confirms, in person, what the party has only inferred from Marrow's neck-graft:** the Silent Ascendency is scaling stolen soul-craft into more than individual grafts — the soul-vessels are a real, ongoing production line, not a rumor.
- **The cores here are early-stage, not finished** — consistent with the harvesting operation still being active, not complete. Nothing here should read as a working weapon or a threat in itself.
- **A courier's logbook**, half-filled in a hand that isn't Reyes's, tracks deliveries in and shipments out by date and weight only — no names, no destination beyond "upriver" for outbound entries. It's a real lead (confirms the pipeline continues past this point) without naming where "upriver" actually goes — that's a future session's thread, not tonight's.
- **The hatch.** At the back of the workroom, under a hinged grate and a lot of accumulated indifference, is a **bolted iron hatch** and the top of a timber stair going down into the rock. It is obviously used. It is obviously not a thirty-minute problem. **This is where tonight stops** — see [[Greyfen Hollow]] for everything below it.
- **DM-only — two things to plant, lightly, and not explain:**
	- One seat at the workbench is *wrong*: an oversized chair, and the stone armrest beside it gouged in four parallel lines by something with claws. There's a whetstone on the bench that no tool in this room needs. Don't draw attention to it; just describe it once and move on. It pays off at [[Izek Strazni (COS)]].

## Secrets and Clues

> [!tip] 13 Secrets
> 1. Crane's ledger and the tally-house's own gapped manifests both point at the same clerk — the pattern was always there to find, not planted for tonight.
> 2. Guildmaster Anselm Corrow is not Ascendency and has no idea his own senior clerk is compromised — his blindness is willful about *something* moving through his docks, not about who.
> 3. Ostwin Reyes has never met the real Crane, or "Velmont," or anyone above him — the whole network above Bruokdon runs on couriers and passwords, nobody meeting anyone.
> 4. Reyes knows the route onward to Greyfen Hollow but genuinely doesn't know what happens there — he's never been curious enough to ask.
> 5. The stonework at Greyfen Hollow's center predates the Silent Ascendency by a wide margin — repurposed, like the Cinderworks, not built for this.
> 6. A single Amber Warden guards the site in person — the party's first physical confirmation of the soul-vessel motif beyond Marrow's corpse.
> 7. The Warden's three amber conduits are both its power source and a destructible weak point — a mechanical and narrative echo of the anti-magic gems from the S43 Stone Golem fight.
> 8. The soul-vessels inside are early-stage, not finished products — the harvesting operation is still active, not near completion.
> 9. A courier's logbook confirms shipments continue "upriver" from the Hollow — the pipeline's true endpoint is still unknown, reserved for a future session (The Convergence, per the campaign bible).
> 10. Marrow's corpse and its uninvestigated amber graft are still sitting in Bruokdon custody, untouched — a standing loose end this session doesn't resolve.
> 11. Faerah's moon pendant is a bearer-mark of an ancient drow order of Eilistraee — one of a set, given to those meant to lead others out of tyranny. Freeing Barovia "woke" it.
> 12. A dusk elf elder in Duskferry was guided out of the Underdark by Faerah's sister — proof the sister's escape work was real, organised, and larger than Faerah ever knew.
> 13. The dusk elves' mourning-verse, and Eilistraee's answer at the shrine, point the same way about Faerah's sister — grief-comfort on its face, something else underneath (payoff reserved for Act II, Arc 2).
