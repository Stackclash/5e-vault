## Roster Tonight
[[Faerah Duskrane]], [[Redgrave]], [[Wisp]], [[Yevelda Ovak]] — level 10, tier 3, 4 active. **[[Roark Battleborn]]'s sheet is run by his own player tonight, but as the antagonist** — see DM Only, below, and hand that player the **Player Handout** section *before* the session, not at the table.

## Strong Start

> [!info] The Umbral Athenaeum, Mid-Sweep
> No recap needed — pick up days after S43. "Roark" rejoined the party a couple of days ago with the (fake, though nobody knows that) heartbeat-detection device in hand, reporting that the handoff with Corwin Vantor went smoothly and that Lyssandra Veil chose to stay behind to run further diagnostics on it with him — she'd catch up. Nobody had reason to doubt it, and the device has been sitting in the party's hands since, possibly even used once or twice on low-stakes test subjects with unremarkable results.
>
> The mass-testing plan is underway: Athenaeum staff, scholars, and hangers-on are being pulled aside in ones and twos to demonstrate magic under Gousse's supervision, guards posted at every exit per the party's own plan. It's tedious, procedural work — exactly the kind of scene where nobody's watching "Roark" too closely, because why would they.
>
> Let the party set the pace of the sweep themselves for a few minutes — who they question, who seems nervous, small color. Then cut to Scene 2 the moment it's earned a beat of quiet.

## Scenes

> [!info] Scene 1 — The Sweep (framing, not a full scene)
> Let this run as long as it's fun and no longer. A few options to season it if the table wants texture: a jumpy junior archivist who fails the cast-a-cantrip test out of sheer nerves (innocent — a nice near-miss before the real reveal); a bored guard captain grumbling about how long this is taking; Gousse working the line efficiently, visibly relieved every clean result. **Roark is not being tested — the plan was always about Athenaeum members, and everyone already knows he can't cast a spell to prove anything either way.** Nobody's looking at him twice. That's the point.

> [!info] Scene 2 — The Discovery

> [!quote] What They See
> A runner — one of the guards posted at the Athenaeum's outer gate — pushes through, out of breath, straight for Gousse. Whatever she says makes him go very still. He looks past her, toward the party, and his eyes land on "Roark" for a half-second too long before he speaks.
>
> "There's been an incident. A man's been found dead near the old millwright's yard, past the north wall — Corwin Vantor, by his papers. Meeting site, by the look of it — torn up bad, signs of a real fight. Guard captain wants to know why Mistress Veil's name is all over his appointment book, and why nobody's seen her in days."

> **What's actually there, if the party goes to look (send them with an escort, or narrate it — table's call):** [[Corwin Vantor]]'s body, killed defending himself; the wrecked remains of his *real* prototype, unmistakably destroyed, not "checked out for diagnostics"; signs of a struggle involving at least three other people, dragged rather than walked out. No sign of Lyssandra. No second device anywhere.
>
> **The gap that should land immediately — no Insight check needed, this is arithmetic, not a read:** "Roark" is standing right there, and the device he brought back and the story he told don't survive contact with this scene. If the party doesn't clock it themselves in the first few seconds, let an NPC say the quiet part — Gousse, a guard, whoever's handy: *"That's not possible. He said the handoff went clean. He said she stayed behind."*

> [!info] Scene 3 — The Frame
> This is Marrow's scene to lose. Cornered without warning, it improvises: deflects, minimizes ("must be a different Battleborn — common enough name"), or — better, if the opening's there — steers suspicion toward whichever nervous Athenaeum member the sweep already flagged as a near-miss in Scene 1. It can lean on a plant already in the crowd if you want the frame to have real teeth (see DM Only).
>
> **[[Martin Thricefold]] is the pressure valve.** He's been quietly watching Roark since his god's warning, paranoid and specific in ways nobody else in the party has reason to be. Let him name the wrongness directly rather than have the party puzzle it out from clues alone — a personal detail flubbed, a reaction a half-second late, the wrong hand reaching for a blade that isn't Uthraad. Play him abrasive and certain, not hedging: *"That's not him. I don't know what that is, but that's not him."*
>
> **Branch — if the party doesn't bite on Martin's accusation:** don't force it. Let "Roark" successfully redirect for now — the scapegoat gets detained, the party moves on, and Marrow gets a clean exit later tonight or off-screen before the next session. This is a real branch, not a soft rail: a missed read here is a worse outcome for the party (Marrow escapes clean, cell survives intact) but not a broken session — it becomes the hook for a colder, better-prepared Marrow encounter down the line, and Martin doesn't let it go quietly, which is its own scene.

> [!info] Scene 4 — The Reveal
> Once the accusation lands and "Roark" has nowhere left to redirect it, it breaks — either bolting for an exit (already guarded, per the sweep's own security posture — no clean escape) or turning to fight rather than be taken. Either way, this goes to combat. See Encounter, below.
>
> **On defeat or incapacitation, Marrow reverts to its true grey doppelganger form immediately** — visual, no roll required. This is the payoff shot of the whole session; give it room.

> [!info] Scene 5 — Aftermath
> Whatever's left standing gets interrogated (see Secrets and Clues). The real information here: Roark and Lyssandra are alive, taken for separate reasons, and being moved along Velmont's old smuggling routes — which means there's a clock. Land the session on that, not on tidy closure. This should feel like the start of the next thing, not the end of this one.

## Secrets and Clues

> [!tip] 10 Secrets — placeable across the interrogation, the crime scene, and the fight itself
> 1. The device "Roark" brought back is fake — inert, cobbled together, never worked. Anyone who actually tests it (on a known human, or just an honest Arcana/Investigation check) finds nothing inside it capable of detecting anything.
> 2. Roark and [[Mistress Lyssandra Veil|Lyssandra Veil]] are alive — captured, not killed.
> 3. They're being moved along [[Lord Caldris Velmont]]'s old smuggling routes — still usable infrastructure even with Velmont exposed. A ticking clock: still in Bruokdon, or already being moved out.
> 4. Lyssandra was taken specifically for her illusion mastery — cultists describe it as "ward-work," nothing more specific. (Seeds a later thread; don't over-explain it if asked.)
> 5. Roark was opportunistic — leverage against the party, not planned in advance.
> 6. The amber shard recovered from Marrow's true form is kin to something Redgrave's own soul has brushed against before — worth an open Insight/Arcana or just letting Redgrave's player notice unprompted.
> 7. Rank-and-file cultists call the graft "the Marrow-rite" — rare, a mark of trust from above, not standard issue.
> 8. Cultists speak of **the Great Unveiling** — a promised revelation of all hidden truths — and of their god only by title: the Whispered One, God of Secrets, the Undying King. Never a name. (The party already knows the name Vecna — this plays as confirmation and dread, not a twist.)
> 9. Nobody caught tonight knows who runs the Ascendency above their own cell — compartmentalized on purpose.
> 10. Marrow was the doppelganger from S43 (Velmont, then Gousse) — it never left Bruokdon, it went to ground and came back through Roark. (This one's less "clue" than confirmation once the reveal happens — let the party connect it themselves if they can.)

## Encounter — The Reveal

> [!warning] "Roark" and the Cell's Cover (Deadly — this is the arc's climax)
> ```encounter
> name: The Reveal
> creatures:
>   - 2: [[Cult Fanatic]]
>   - 1: [[Gladia(TOR), Ascendency Enforcer]]
>   - 2: [[Cult Fanatic, Reinforcement]], hidden
> ```
> **Add Marrow to the tracker by hand as a hostile, using [[Roark Battleborn]]'s own character sheet directly (137 HP, AC 15, all his normal Barbarian features), plus one new trait — *Grey Flesh, Twisted Bone*: once this fight, when reduced to 0 HP, Marrow drops to 1 instead. Do not use [[Marrow]]'s bestiary block tonight.** The two hidden **Cult Fanatic (Reinforcement)** entries are cell muscle seeded in the Scene 1 sweep crowd — reveal and add them to the tracker the moment Marrow is first bloodied (≤68 HP) or an alarm is raised, whichever comes first. See DM Only for why and for the escape trigger.
>
> **CR math (Lazy DM benchmark):** 4 active PCs × level 10 = 40 total levels; potentially deadly at summed CR > 20 (half, at 5th level+). "Roark," run off his own sheet rather than a CR, doesn't plug cleanly into this math — worked defensive/offensive CR separately below.
> - **Defensive:** 137 HP at AC 15, with resistance to nonmagical bludgeoning/piercing/slashing while raging. Effective HP against the party's likely mixed magic/nonmagic weapons lands around 160–180 — roughly CR 7–8 by the durability table.
> - **Offensive:** raging, reckless-attacking Berserker with Frenzy — 3 attacks/round (Extra Attack + Frenzy bonus action), roughly 24–29 damage/round after accounting for advantage from Reckless Attack. That's CR 4–5 territory, lower than the HP total makes it feel.
> - ***Grey Flesh, Twisted Bone*** (new): a once-per-fight "shrug off a killing blow," functionally a single legendary resistance against being dropped. This is what pushes Marrow's effective rating up — round-1 novas shouldn't end the arc's climax early.
> - **Verdict:** call it CR 8 equivalent (up from the original CR 6–7 read). Add the starting backup — 2× Cult Fanatic (CR 2 each = 4) + 1× Gladiator reskinned as an Ascendency Enforcer (CR 5) = 9 — for 17 up front. The hidden 2× Cult Fanatic (Reinforcement) (CR 2 each = 4) bring the full total to **21 CR against a deadly threshold of 20** once they reveal. Genuinely Deadly on paper this time, matching the billing — run it like the arc finale it is.
>
> **Action economy:** round 1 is 4 enemies (Marrow + 2 Fanatics + Gladiator) vs. 4 PCs — even, same principle as the original design. Once the reinforcement wave reveals, that becomes 6 vs. 4 — a real crescendo rather than flat difficulty the whole way through. That's also the point where Wisp (AC 14, HP 42) is most exposed to getting ganged up on — worth a heads-up to that player about positioning once the fight's back half opens up.
>
> **Terrain:** the Athenaeum's testing hall — desks and record-shelves for cover, a crowd of civilian scholars and staff to complicate area effects (nobody wants Wisp's fireball catching bystanders), guards already posted at every exit per the party's own security plan from S43 (this is what removes Marrow's clean escape route and forces the fight). The two reinforcement Cult Fanatics are already in the room, indistinguishable from Scene 1's sweep hangers-on until they act — no entrance to stage, just reveal them on the tracker when they turn.
>
> **Tactics:** Cult Fanatics open with Command/Cause Fear to control the party's positioning and protect Marrow's flank rather than engaging directly. The Gladiator ("Ascendency Enforcer") plants itself between Marrow and whoever's hitting hardest — Shield Bash to prone a melee threat, Parry to shrug off the follow-up, Multiattack (3 attacks) to punish anyone who closes anyway. Marrow itself fights to control the room, not to trade blows evenly — going for whoever looks squishiest (Wisp) if it's losing the initiative war, or for the nearest exit if one opens up. The instant Marrow is bloodied (≤68 HP) or an alarm goes up, reveal the two hidden Cult Fanatic (Reinforcement) — they were sweep "civilians" the whole time, and their turn should land as its own small gut-punch beat before combat continues.
> **Morale:** Cult Fanatics (both waves) fight to the death (true believers) unless clearly overwhelmed and given a real chance to surrender — a few might, and are worth taking alive for the ledger. The Gladiator is a professional, not a zealot — it breaks and runs below ~25% HP if it can, which is a fair tell if the party wants to read it that way. Marrow's own morale/escape trigger, including how *Grey Flesh, Twisted Bone* feeds into it, is in DM Only.

## Player Handout — For Roark's Player, Before the Session

> [!quote] Give this to them directly (text, print, whatever's easiest) ahead of time — not at the table.
>
> **You're not playing Roark tonight — you're playing [[Marrow]], a doppelganger currently disguised as him.** Run Roark's character sheet exactly as written, full stop — Marrow is genuinely borrowing his combat ability, so there's no mechanical difference. This is about how you play the roleplay side.
>
> **What Marrow knows:**
> - He's the Silent Ascendency's cell leader in Bruokdon — not a foot soldier. He captured the real Lord Caldris Velmont months ago and has been running Velmont's smuggling ring personally from inside that stolen identity ever since.
> - Redgrave's Geas blew that cover (S43). Marrow briefly wore Professor Gousse Windcaller's face to buy a few more minutes, then slipped away into a crowd rather than run from the city outright.
> - Roark and Lyssandra Veil left the Athenaeum together, unescorted, to collect a device from an independent monster-hunter, Corwin Vantor. Marrow's people ambushed you — opportunistic, not planned in advance; the Ascendency didn't know the device existed until that day. Corwin fought back and was killed. Lyssandra was taken because her illusion mastery is valuable to the cell for future work. Roark was taken as insurance — and, worn as a disguise, as a way back into the party that's been dismantling the operation.
> - Marrow put on Roark and came back to the party with a **fake device** and a cover story: the handoff went smoothly, Lyssandra chose to stay behind with Corwin to run further diagnostics, she'll catch up. That lie is standing, unchallenged, as tonight opens.
> - Priorities, in order: protect the cover and the cell; if that fails, protect himself (deflect, frame someone else, flee); fighting the party is the last resort, not the first move.
> - He's a genuine, if pragmatic, believer in the Ascendency's doctrine of "the Great Unveiling", his one goal is to recover his cover in the Umbral Atheneaum
>
> **What Marrow does NOT know — play these as real gaps, not lies to cover:**
> - Roark's actual personal history in any depth: his father, his bond with Martin Thricefold, specific memories, backstory, inside jokes. The graft gives Marrow physical instinct and surface behavior, not Roark's memories. If a question is personal or specific, **deflect or go vague — don't confidently invent details that could be checked later.**
> - Anything the real Roark experienced, said, or thought before capture. No access to that.
> - The party's plans or conversations from before this errand, beyond what an attentive person embedded with them the last couple of days would plausibly know.
> - Whether the mass-testing sweep is going to work. Marrow's improvising that risk same as everyone else.
>
> **How to play the pressure, in order:** minimize → redirect suspicion elsewhere → fight only when genuinely cornered with no other move. If someone casts something that might see through the disguise (Zone of Truth, detect magic, etc.), pause and let the DM tell you privately what it reveals — don't decide that yourself.
>
> Small "tells" (a mannerism slightly off, a beat too calm) are fun if you want them, but you're not obligated to sabotage your own cover — whether anyone catches on is the table's job. This is built to land as a reveal — don't force it early, and follow the DM's lead in the moment it breaks.

## DM Only — Do Not Read Aloud

> [!danger] The full picture
> [[Marrow]] is the Silent Ascendency's Bruokdon cell leader — the same doppelganger that impersonated [[Lord Caldris Velmont]] and then [[Professor Gousse Windcaller]] in S43. It never left the city. After Redgrave's Geas burned its cover, it regrouped and ambushed Roark and Lyssandra opportunistically (not premeditated — the Ascendency had no advance knowledge of [[Corwin Vantor]]'s device, and Vantor had no personal tie to Roark at all — he was Lyssandra's contact, and Roark simply escorted her; Vantor's death was collateral, not the objective). Lyssandra was taken for her illusion mastery (future ward-work, seeding "The Convergence," Act I Arc 6 — stay vague if asked); Roark was taken as leverage and as a door back into the party.
>
> **The fake device is already in play going into tonight, established as having happened a couple of days ago:** Marrow, as "Roark," already returned to the party with an inert decoy and a cover story — the handoff went fine, Lyssandra stayed behind with Corwin to run diagnostics. That lie is what the crime scene in Scene 2 contradicts; you don't need Martin's read to trigger suspicion, the story and the evidence simply don't match, which makes the "party doesn't bite" branch in Scene 3 about whether they *act* on an obvious contradiction, not whether they *notice* one. Martin's accusation is confirmation and momentum, not the sole trigger. See [[Marrow]]'s note for the full mechanism (the Marrow-rite graft) and [[The Hunt for Vecna]]'s Active Threads for the campaign-level version of this lore.
>
> Make sure Roark's player has the **Player Handout** section above *before* the session — the fake-device cover story only works at the table if they're already playing from it, not improvising it cold.
>
> **The scapegoat (optional, play by ear):** if you want Marrow's frame attempt to have real teeth in Scene 3, one of the Scene 1 sweep participants — nameless, invent on the spot, doesn't need stats — is quietly an Ascendency sympathizer Marrow can nod toward or has already primed to "confess." Don't pre-write this one; whether it exists and how far it goes should depend on how Scene 3 is actually playing at the table. **This is separate from the two hidden Cult Fanatic (Reinforcement) in the encounter block** — the scapegoat is an unarmed civilian sympathizer for the Scene 3 frame; the reinforcements are armed cell muscle seeded in the same crowd, held hidden on the tracker until Marrow is bloodied. Both can exist in the same scene without stepping on each other.
>
> **Grey Flesh, Twisted Bone:** Marrow's new once-per-fight trait (see Encounter, above) — when it would drop to 0 HP, it drops to 1 instead. Treat the moment it triggers as effectively synonymous with the ⅓-HP escape threshold below: if the party doesn't immediately follow up and finish the job, that's your cleanest possible cue for Marrow to burn its action on Shapechanger next turn rather than keep fighting at 1 HP.
>
> **Marrow's escape trigger:** no scripted flee — Marrow fights for real. But if Roark's sheet drops to roughly ⅓ of his max HP (≈46) or lower — including immediately after *Grey Flesh, Twisted Bone* saves it at 1 HP — or if Marrow gets a genuinely clean opening (an unguarded exit, a crowd to vanish into), it can use its own innate Shapechanger trait as an action to drop the "Roark" disguise entirely, take the form of a random nearby bystander, and attempt to flee — the same trick it used against Faerah and Nyxia in S43. This is a real option, not a bluff; let it work if the party doesn't shut the door fast enough. Whether it escapes decides how this arc closes:
> - **Captured or killed:** the Bruokdon cell loses its leader — effectively decapitates the local Silent Ascendency operation and gives Act I, Arc 2 ("Breather: Bruokdon aftermath") real closure before the party moves on to the Great Library.
> - **Escapes:** Marrow survives as a recurring personal nemesis, the cell keeps functioning under damaged leadership, and this becomes unfinished business layered under the Roark/Lyssandra rescue.
> Either is a good outcome — don't steer it. Update [[Marrow]]'s `condition` field and the campaign bible's Active Threads once you know which way it went.
>
> **After the session:** update [[Marrow]]'s and [[Mistress Lyssandra Veil]]'s `condition`/notes, log Roark's and Lyssandra's captivity as a new open thread (likely a quest note once the rescue actually gets planned — don't build it before it's needed), and note whether the scapegoat thread left any loose ends (an innocent detained, a sympathizer still loose). The interrogation results and Marrow's fate should also update [[The Hunt for Vecna]]'s Active Threads.
